## Problem Statement
A for-fun side project: a single-player tactical wargame where the player commands infantry and artillery through pre-battle doctrine rather than direct real-time unit control. The enemy is AI-controlled. Drones (recon / artillery spotting) are planned for a later phase, deferred until the core loop is proven.

## What Makes This Cool
The player never issues per-unit move orders during battle. Instead, they set standing doctrine before the fight (retreat thresholds, "shoot and scoot" vs. accept counter-battery risk, initial dispositions), and units interpret that doctrine autonomously mid-fight. This sits between full RTS micromanagement and Combat Mission's WEGO order-then-watch model — closer to real mission command (auftragstaktik) than existing wargames implement. Drones later become the recon/targeting layer that makes delegated decisions consequential.

## Constraints
- Single-player only; enemy is AI-controlled (not multiplayer, not co-op).
- No map/scenario polish, no drones, no armor/air in the first build — core combat loop only (movement, line-of-sight, artillery timing/accuracy, morale/retreat).
- For-fun side project — no store/distribution pipeline needed yet.
- Battle end condition: one side fully routed (all surviving squads have crossed their own retreat threshold — simplified to a status flag, not a requirement to actually path to the map edge) or destroyed, or a fixed time limit (e.g. 5 minutes) elapses — whichever comes first. If the time limit is reached with neither side routed/destroyed, the battle ends in a draw.
- Minimal terrain: a handful of static rectangular obstacles on the map that block LOS raycasts and confer a cover bonus (reduced hit chance) to units behind them. No destructible terrain, no elevation.
- Minimal casualty model: each squad is a fixed number of pips (e.g. 4 per infantry squad, 1 per mortar crew); each resolved hit removes one pip. The "casualties exceeded 30% threshold" doctrine check is evaluated against pips lost, not a continuous stat.
- Suppression is explicitly deferred, not built into v1 (Codex's recommendation named it, but it's cut here along with ballistics dispersion and continuous attrition — see Approach A's Feasibility note). Morale/retreat in v1 is driven purely by pip loss vs. threshold, not a separate suppression stat.

## Premises
1. The doctrine-delegation mechanic (standing orders + risk tolerance, not direct unit control) is the real differentiator — more than drones.
2. A minimal slice (movement + LOS + artillery timing/accuracy, no map polish, no drones) is enough to test whether that mechanic is fun.
3. If the core loop isn't fun without drones, drones won't fix it — so drones stay deferred until the core loop is proven.
4. This is a for-fun side project — no distribution/store planning needed yet, just something playable to show wargaming hobbyists eventually.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Minimal Godot prototype (chosen)
Custom-built in Godot 4: two infantry squads + one mortar per side, a fixed-behavior scripted AI enemy (not a mirrored doctrine engine — see Next Steps), LOS raycasts against static obstacles, pip-based morale/retreat logic, doctrine panel. No map polish, no drones.
- Effort: M. Risk: Low-Medium after the v1 cuts below (the original uncut scope — full ballistics, continuous attrition, generative log — was underestimated at S/M and higher-risk; see Feasibility note).
- Pros: full control over the bespoke doctrine mechanic; produces something visual and playable to show wargaming hobbyists.
- Cons: enemy AI is a simple scripted first pass, not deeply adaptive.
- Feasibility note: the first review pass flagged that movement + LOS + morale/suppression + a doctrine engine + artillery ballistics + a decision-explaining log is more than a literal weekend at full fidelity. v1 cuts further: fixed mortar reload/relocate cooldown instead of real ballistics dispersion, pip-based squad casualties instead of continuous attrition, and a templated (not generative) combat log. Richer versions are fast-follow iteration after the core loop is validated once.

### Approach B: Fork OpenRA (ruled out for now)
Mod the open-source OpenRA RTS engine: replace click-to-command with a doctrine/intent layer, inherit its combat sim, pathfinding, and fog-of-war.
- Effort: L/XL. Risk: Medium-High.
- Ruled out because OpenRA's whole interaction model (direct unit orders) fights the exact mechanic that differentiates this game — disproportionate effort for a side project's first slice.

### Approach C: Text-log spike (ruled out for now)
Headless tick-based simulation, no graphics — resolves doctrine into outcomes and prints a combat log.
- Effort: XS/S. Risk: Very low.
- Ruled out because the user chose a small custom game with real graphics instead — wants something visual and playable, not just a numeric spike.

## Recommended Approach
Approach A: Minimal Godot prototype. It directly tests the core doctrine-delegation loop with real visual/spatial feel, is achievable in about a weekend, and produces something that can actually be demoed to wargaming hobbyists later.

## Success Criteria
Primary (subjective): after watching a squad retreat or hold under fire, does the player immediately want to revise the doctrine they gave it? If yes, the core mechanic has proven itself, independent of drones or visual polish.
Secondary (concrete): across at least 3 solo playthroughs, the player changes at least one doctrine setting between playthroughs in at least 2 of the 3 — a countable signal that the mechanic is generating real decisions, not just being watched passively.

## Combat Resolution — Minimal Spec
- Movement/engagement: squads and mortars are stationary at their deployed position for the whole battle (aside from a threshold-triggered retreat, per the Constraints' status-flag rule) and auto-fire at any enemy unit within LOS. No player-directed movement, no advance-toward-enemy behavior in v1.
- Hit chance: each firing unit has a fixed base hit probability per shot (e.g. infantry 20%, mortar 40% against a target it has LOS to — "spotted" means the same LOS raycast check used for infantry, no separate indirect-fire spotting system in v1), modified by a flat cover multiplier (e.g. x0.5) if the LOS raycast to the target crosses a static obstacle. No range falloff or line-of-fire cone in v1 — a shot either has LOS to its target or it doesn't.
- Mortar "shoot and scoot": reload is a fixed constant (e.g. 3s) separate from the player-set relocate-cooldown value. While in shoot-and-scoot mode, a mortar MUST relocate after every shot fired — it cannot fire again until relocate-cooldown + reload completes. While in "hold position" mode, a mortar skips relocation and can fire again as soon as reload alone completes, but each shot fired from a stationary position adds to a rising counter-battery chance: after firing without relocating for longer than a threshold (e.g. 2 consecutive shots), the enemy AI gains a flat 5%-per-1-second-tick chance to target that mortar's last-known firing position with its own artillery. This is the concrete risk/reward the doctrine toggle trades off.

## Doctrine Panel & Combat Log — Minimal Spec
- Doctrine panel: a simple pre-battle screen with numeric fields / sliders per squad — retreat-at-pip-loss threshold and a deploy-zone pick (one of a few pre-marked starting positions on the map, not free placement) — and per mortar — the same deploy-zone pick, plus a "hold position" vs. "shoot and scoot" toggle and the relocate-cooldown value. Edited only before the battle starts; no in-battle doctrine changes in v1.
- Combat log: templated, event-triggered text (string interpolation on trigger conditions, e.g. `"{squad} withdrew: {pips_lost}/{pips_total} casualties exceeded {threshold}"`) — not generative/NLG. Scrubbable list, newest entry at top.

## Revision Log

### 2026-09-04 — v1 implementation feedback, six changes

After the first playable prototype (Godot 4, matching Next Steps above), user feedback drove a substantial rework, implemented directly rather than through a full office-hours pass:

1. **Terrain & elevation.** Replaced the flat 2-obstacle "cover only" map with typed terrain zones (`GameConfig.TERRAIN_ZONES`): a village (BUILDING) sitting on a hill (HIGH_GROUND) that the player holds, plus TREES zones in the open ground the enemy crosses. Elevation (0 = lowland, 1 = high ground) extends detection range for whoever holds it (+150px). BUILDING/TREES now separately affect concealment (spot chance) and cover (hit chance once spotted).
2. **Spotting.** Units are invisible to the opposing side (`Unit.is_spotted`) until detected. Detection is a per-tick probability roll (`CombatResolver.roll_spot`) based on range, elevation, terrain concealment, and whether the target is moving. Only spotted units can be fired upon (`Unit.is_targetable()`). No "losing" a spot once gained — deliberate simplification to keep state legible.
3. **Enemy strength increased.** Enemy is now 3 squads + 1 mortar vs. the player's 2 squads + 1 mortar — numeric superiority via unit count, not per-unit stat buffs, for legibility.
4. **After-action report, not win/lose.** Battle end now computes held-or-not (did the player avoid being fully routed) and an exchange ratio (enemy pips lost ÷ player pips lost), producing one of four verdicts: SUCCESSFUL DEFENSE, PYRRHIC DEFENSE, COSTLY WITHDRAWAL (favorable exchange even without holding), or DEFEAT. Thresholds (1.5:1 for a clean defense, 2.0:1 for a favorable withdrawal) are a first guess, not validated against actual play.
5. **Mortar model corrected.** Removed the mortar's retreat-threshold doctrine control entirely — a mortar crew (1 pip) is either in action or knocked out by a single hit, never worn down by percent casualties the way a squad is. A knocked-out mortar gets a flavor-only crew-casualty percentage (25-100%, random) for the AAR report; it has no mechanical effect.
6. **Defense advantages via movement + firing-reveals-position.** Enemy squads now physically advance from the map edge toward the village (straight-line, no pathfinding) until reaching engagement range, then hold or retreat; the enemy mortar stays put. Movement sets `Unit.Activity.MOVING`, tripling spot chance (`GameConfig.MOVING_SPOT_MULTIPLIER`). Firing a shot unconditionally reveals the firer's position (`is_spotted = true`) regardless of terrain, on both sides symmetrically.

**Verified before handoff:** a headless Godot simulation harness (written for verification, not shipped) ran the full battle loop end-to-end across multiple random seeds with no runtime errors, producing sensible and varied outcomes (holds, defeats, and a favorable-exchange withdrawal all observed).

**Not yet tuned:** all new numeric constants (detection range, spot chance, elevation bonus, enemy advance speed, AAR verdict thresholds) are first-pass estimates. Expect to adjust them after actually playing it.

### 2026-09-04 — v2 implementation feedback, six more changes

1. **Believable map.** Terrain now renders as an actual map instead of flat tinted rectangles: hills get contour-ring shading, the village gets individual building shapes with roofs, forests get a scattered tree-dot pattern, and a road cuts across the open ground (currently decorative only — no movement bonus yet). Shared between the battle view and the deployment screen via `GameConfig.draw_terrain()` so they always match.
2. **Drag-and-drop deployment.** Replaced the zone dropdown with `DeploymentScreen` — a real map view where you drag each of your 3 squads + mortar to a starting position inside `GameConfig.PLAYER_DEPLOYMENT_ZONE` (outlined in yellow) before the battle starts. Doctrine sliders (retreat threshold, mortar shoot-and-scoot) moved to a sidebar; `main.gd` combines both into the doctrine dict on Start Battle.
3. **General retreat order.** Added `BattleManager.order_general_retreat()`, wired to an "Order General Retreat" button visible throughout the battle. Forces every ACTIVE player unit into `Unit.State.RETREATING` immediately, regardless of casualties. Added a fourth unit state, `WITHDRAWN`, reached when a retreating unit (either side) physically reaches its own safe line — only then does it stop counting as "still fighting." Battle end now waits for all retreats to actually finish before generating the report.
4. **Stronger, sharper defense advantage.** A unit caught `MOVING` now loses its terrain cover entirely and takes fire at 1.6x the normal chance (`GameConfig.MOVING_HIT_MULTIPLIER`) — moving is genuinely dangerous, not just easier to spot. Enemy squads also get a two-tier casualty reaction: past a 25% casualty threshold they log "reports heavy resistance" (flavor only), past 50% they actually retreat.
5. **Fire log removed.** No more per-shot hit/miss entries. The log now only records spotting, revealing-by-fire, threshold/ordered retreats, reaching safety, going out of action, and counter-battery hits.
6. **Force ratio widened to 3 v 6.** Player: 3 squads + 1 mortar (was 2). Enemy: 6 squads + 1 mortar (was 3) — deliberately long odds. "Held" is now judged the instant no player unit remains ACTIVE (an ordered retreat gives up the position immediately, even before the withdrawal physically completes), while the AAR's exchange-ratio verdict tiers (unchanged from the v1 revision) already cover "retreated clean after inflicting casualties" as a TACTICAL WITHDRAWAL, separate from DEFEAT.

**Verified before handoff:** re-ran the headless simulation harness (written for verification, not shipped) across two scenarios — a full fight with no manual retreat, and one with a general retreat ordered mid-battle — confirming no runtime errors, correct WITHDRAWN transitions, casualties taken during withdrawal, and the concern-threshold flavor log firing correctly.

**Not yet verified:** the actual drag-and-drop interaction (mouse-down/motion/up on the deployment screen) — headless automation doesn't simulate real input well, so this needs eyes-on testing. All new constants (moving-hit multiplier, concern threshold, retreat speeds/safe lines, deployment zone bounds) are first-pass estimates.

### 2026-09-04 — v3 implementation feedback, cover/mortar/movement rework

1. **More scattered cover.** Added two more small tree clumps flanking the road and two small isolated wall/ruin cover spots in the open ground, alongside the existing village and forest zones — the enemy now has real intermediate cover to fall back on, not just the village itself.
2. **Defenders hit harder from the start.** Player squads now fire at 0.32 base hit chance vs. the enemy's 0.20 — prepared, pre-ranged defensive positions land their first shots far more often. Mortars unaffected (indirect fire competency isn't a defense/offense asymmetry).
3. **Road march, then disperse.** Enemy squads now start with a shared movement-target system (`Unit.move_target`/`has_move_target`, replacing the old x-only advance) and initially march toward a shared rally point on the road. The first hit any enemy squad takes (of any kind, not just mortar) makes it break off toward the nearest cover zone permanently (`Unit.sought_cover`, one-time). Verified via a 200-trial isolated test that this fires correctly and doesn't re-trigger.
4. **Weapon-specific cover.** Split cover into `SQUAD_COVER_MULTIPLIER` (strong: 0.15 in buildings, 0.35 in trees) and `MORTAR_COVER_MULTIPLIER` (weak: 0.7 buildings, 0.85 trees) — direct fire struggles against cover, mortars punch through it. A moving target still loses cover entirely either way (unchanged from the prior round).
5. **Fire log removed** was already done last round; this round removed the routine per-shot text entirely from consideration and replaced it with a **visual tracer**: every shot (including indirect mortar fire with no LOS) draws a brief color-coded line + muzzle flash (`BattleManager._fire_flashes`) — yellow for enemy fire, cyan for player fire, fading over 0.3s. This is the "make it clear when the enemy is firing" requirement.
6. **Mortar-fire relocation.** A squad hit specifically by mortar fire (not direct fire) now has an independent 35% chance to bolt for the nearest cover (`Unit.bolted_for_cover`), regardless of overall casualties — verified via a 200-trial isolated test (72/200 ≈ 36%, matching the 35% target) once the automatic retreat-threshold priority (which correctly pre-empts this on a severe hit) was isolated out of the test.
7. **Mortars decoupled from direct-fire spotting.** A mortar's own fire no longer auto-reveals it to enemy squads the way a squad's muzzle flash does (`_tick_fire` only sets `is_spotted` for SQUAD kind) — it can still be found via the normal detection roll, just not automatically by firing. A mortar's targeting is now unrestricted by range (`_pick_target` only applies `SQUAD_ENGAGEMENT_RANGE` to SQUAD kind) — it fires on anything any friendly unit has spotted, anywhere, matching spotter-relayed indirect fire.
8. **Mortar-vs-mortar counter-battery**, replacing the old per-tick accrual system entirely: every time a mortar fires, the *opposing side's mortar(s) specifically* — not any other unit — get a chance to hit back (`GameConfig.MORTAR_COUNTER_BATTERY_HOLD_CHANCE` = 0.22 vs. `..._SCOOT_CHANCE` = 0.06). Mortars are now explicitly high-priority targets for each other, and shoot-and-scoot meaningfully lowers that risk.
9. **Enemy gets two mortars** (`GameConfig.ENEMY_MORTAR_POSITIONS`), player keeps one.
10. **Shoot-and-scoot visibly moves the mortar** — a short random hop (`GameConfig.MORTAR_SCOOT_HOP_DISTANCE` = 40px) after every shot in that mode, clamped to stay on the map.

**Bug caught and fixed during verification:** `nearest_cover_point` originally returned the exact same zone-center coordinate for every caller, so multiple squads converging on "the nearest cover" all stacked on the identical pixel. Fixed to return a randomized point within the zone instead.

**Also fixed:** one of the three default player squad deployment positions ((190, 430)) fell just outside the village's actual cover footprint (which ends at y=410) — moved to (190, 400).

**Verified before handoff:** full battle simulations (headless) plus two targeted 200-trial isolated unit tests (mortar-hit relocation chance, and the same check after a squad had already sought cover once) confirming both probability-gated behaviors fire at their configured rates with no runtime errors.

**Not yet tuned / observed but not changed:** with both mortars destroyed and both sides dug into strong cover, one test battle ran the full 5-minute time limit without resolving — a legitimate consequence of "cover should be hard to crack," not treated as a bug, but worth knowing about if battles feel like they drag once the mortars are gone.

Player deployment zone grew to match.

No change needed — this was already the highest-priority reaction after retreat-threshold checks.

Verified directly: open-vs-building is now a **13x** swing in hit chance (was ~6.7x). Mortar cover multipliers got the same open-exposure penalty (1.3x) but kept their existing weak trees/building reduction (0.85/0.7) — mortars still punch through cover, unchanged from last round.
4. **Cover status is now visible.** Added `GameConfig.draw_cover_ring()` — a colored ring around every unit (green = in cover, red = exposed) — drawn on units during the battle AND on tokens during deployment (`UnitToken`), updating live as you drag or as units move. It gets +120px detection range on its own spotting rolls and is itself 2x harder for the enemy to spot (0.5x concealment multiplier) — a trained observer that stays hidden. Deploys via its own token on the deployment screen, in a separate, more forward zone (`PLAYER_SPOTTER_DEPLOYMENT_ZONE`, outlined in cyan) than the squads/mortar (outlined in yellow) — reconnaissance doesn't belong in the same spot as firing positions. Player-side only this round; the enemy doesn't get one.

**Bug fixed while wiring up the spotter:** it briefly would have fired every single frame (fire_interval defaulted to 0 for it) before an explicit `kind == SPOTTER` guard was added as the first line of `_tick_fire` — caught before it ever ran, not from a failed test.

**Verified before handoff:** headless tests confirmed the 13x open-vs-building multiplier directly from the tables, confirmed the spotter never appears as a fire-flash source or otherwise fires across a 60-second simulated stretch, and ran a full battle to confirm the AAR report folds the spotter into casualty accounting without special-casing (it shows up as "Player Spotter" in the losses list like any other unit, using the default "was destroyed" wording since it isn't a mortar).

**Not yet tuned:** whether 13x is the right number, whether the spotter's range/concealment bonuses are well-calibrated, and whether giving only the player a spotter (not the enemy) is the right asymmetry — all first-pass judgment calls, easy to adjust once played.

### 2026-09-04 — v5 implementation feedback: LOS blocking, safer retreats, hidden spotter, less clustering

1. **Fixed: units firing through buildings.** Direct fire (squads) had no line-of-sight check at all — only the defender's own terrain mattered, so a squad could shoot straight through the village at anything spotted within range. Added `GameConfig.has_direct_los()` (segment-vs-building-rect intersection, reusing the same geometry the original v1 prototype had before the terrain-zone rewrite dropped it) and gated `BattleManager._pick_target` on it for SQUAD kind only — a building between attacker and target now blocks the shot outright, not just reduces its odds. Mortars are unaffected (indirect, spotter-relayed, as established). Verified directly: a shot straight through the village is blocked, a shot in open ground is clear, and firing out of your own building isn't self-blocked.
2. **Retreats now route through cover first.** `Unit.order_retreat()` checks whether the unit is currently in cover; if not, it first heads for the nearest cover zone (reusing the seek_cover system) before making the final dash to the safe line — a two-leg retreat instead of a beeline. Combined with fix #1, a unit that reaches cover mid-retreat can actually break direct-fire LOS from a pursuer, not just get a probability discount. A unit already in cover skips straight to the final leg. Verified both branches directly.
3. **Spotter deployment opened up + concealment overhauled.** The spotter can now be placed almost anywhere on the map (`PLAYER_SPOTTER_DEPLOYMENT_ZONE` widened from a 220x300 forward patch to nearly the full battlefield). Its concealment is now two different rules depending on terrain: hidden in trees or a building, the enemy's effective detection range against it collapses to 70px flat (overriding elevation bonuses entirely) — "unless they get very close." Standing in the open, it's found close to normally. Verified: 0/500 detections from 150px away while hidden, versus a real (if still low) chance from 40px away.
4. **Less bunching.** `nearest_cover_point` now picks among the 2-3 nearest cover zones with weighted randomness (55%/30%/15%) instead of always the single closest, on top of last round's within-zone randomization — verified 30 distinct coarse-bucket outcomes across 300 calls from one origin. The enemy's initial road-rally waypoints also got a much wider random spread (±60px x / ±120px y, up from a tight deterministic ±8px formula) so the six squads don't march down the road in a near-single-file line to begin with.

**Scoping note:** the LOS-blocking fix applies to firing/targeting only, not to spotting/detection — a squad still can't shoot through a building, but detection remains pure range+concealment with no geometry check. Not reported as broken, so left alone; flagging in case it should be extended for consistency.

**Verified before handoff:** targeted headless tests for all four fixes (LOS blocking in three configurations, hidden-vs-exposed spotter detection at two ranges over 500 trials each, both retreat-order branches, and cover-selection spread over 300 trials), plus one full battle run end-to-end with no runtime errors.

### 2026-09-04 — v5 follow-up: LOS blocking extended to spotting, not just firing

User correction: the previous round's scoping note flagged that LOS-blocking only applied to firing, leaving spotting able to "see" through buildings. ### 2026-09-04 — v6 implementation feedback: road march, mortar tracer visuals, spotter confirmation

1. **Fixed: enemy wasn't actually marching along the road.** Last round's fix for squad-clustering (a wide ±60/±120px random spread on the single road-rally waypoint) had an unintended side effect: many squads' waypoints landed well off the road strip (y:335-359) entirely, so they cut diagonally across open ground instead of visibly marching down the road. Replaced the single scattered waypoint with a real two-leg path (`Unit.set_path()` + a `move_queue`): enter the road near the spawn edge, then march west along it to the engagement point, with each squad's road-y gently staggered (338-356, safely inside the 335-359 strip) so all six are visually distinct without ever leaving the road. Verified directly: 600 position samples taken during the march phase, 0 found off the road strip.
2. **Fixed: fire visually "going through buildings."** This turned out to be specifically about mortar tracers: mortar fire is deliberately LOS-independent (by design, and explicitly reconfirmed by the user this round), but the tracer effect drew every shot — mortar included — as a straight line from firer to target. When a mortar hit a target hidden behind a building (which it's supposed to be able to do), the straight tracer visually cut right through the wall, reading exactly like "impossible" direct fire even though the underlying targeting was correct. Direct (squad) fire was re-verified to have zero LOS violations — the bug was purely in how mortar shots were drawn, not in targeting logic. Mortar tracers now draw as a lobbed arc (`BattleManager._draw_arc_tracer`, a quadratic Bezier curve) instead of a straight line, so indirect fire reads as indirect. Verified: 118 squad shots checked across a full battle, 0 LOS violations; mortar shots (34 observed) are unaffected by the check by design and now render as arcs.
3. **Confirmed already working, no change needed:** "anyone in the view of the spotter should be subject to mortar fire, even without the mortar's own LOS" was already the existing behavior — a mortar's `_pick_target` has never had a range or LOS restriction of its own, and the spotter already participates as a normal spotter in `_spot_side`. Verified directly with an isolated scenario (spotter and target close together with clear mutual LOS, mortar deep in the village) — once the spotter found the target, it became targetable by the mortar regardless of the mortar's own sightline.

### 2026-09-04 — v7: mortar fire now requires a LIVE observer, not just a past sighting

`Unit.is_spotted` never expires once set (deliberate, so the human player keeps seeing an enemy once revealed) — but that made it a poor gate for mortar fire specifically: a target glimpsed once and then broken contact behind a building remained shellable forever, since nothing ever re-checked whether anyone could *currently* see it.

Added `CombatResolver.has_live_observer(target, friendlies)` — mirrors `roll_spot`'s range/elevation/spotter-bonus rules (minus the concealment chance roll) to ask "could some friendly unit be watching this right now," not "was it ever noticed." `BattleManager._pick_target` now requires this for MORTAR kind specifically (still not the mortar's own sight — any friendly unit's current, in-range, unobstructed line of sight qualifies), on top of the existing `is_targetable()` check. Squads are unaffected — they already require their own live LOS+range every tick.

**Verified:** a target spotted once (sticky flag true) then moved out of everyone's detection range — `is_targetable()` correctly stays true (the human player still sees it), but `has_live_observer()` correctly flips to false, meaning the mortar can no longer select it. Confirmed direct-LOS and out-of-range friendlies both correctly fail the check, and a friendly with current clear sight correctly passes it. Also re-ran a full battle end-to-end — mortars still fired 28 shots and the battle resolved normally, confirming the tightened gate doesn't break normal play.

### 2026-09-04 — v8: visibility is now fully live, not a permanent flag (supersedes v7's mortar-only fix)

User correction on v7: the fix there only gated *mortar targeting* against stale sightings via a dedicated check — the underlying `is_spotted` flag itself was still permanent (a design decision from several rounds back, explicitly documented as deliberate at the time: "no losing a spot once gained, to keep this legible"). The user overruled that: a unit should be visible or not visible at a given moment, full stop, for everyone and everything — not just for mortar targeting purposes.

Removed `Unit.is_spotted` entirely, replaced with `Unit.is_visible` — a fact recomputed live every tick, not a latch. `BattleManager._refresh_visibility` (replacing `_spot_side`) now does two things each tick instead of one: a target already visible stays visible only as long as `CombatResolver.has_live_observer` still holds (hard cutoff, no roll — the moment nobody has live line of sight, it goes invisible again); a target not currently visible gets the existing probabilistic `roll_spot` chance to be freshly noticed. Firing still instantly reveals a squad (unchanged), but that too is now subject to being lost again on the very next tick if no enemy unit is actually in range/LOS to have seen the muzzle flash.

Extracted `CombatResolver.effective_detection_range()` so `roll_spot` (gaining visibility) and `has_live_observer` (keeping it) use identical range rules — including the spotter's hidden-in-cover override — so the two can never disagree about how far someone can be seen.

This made v7's mortar-specific `has_live_observer` check in `_pick_target` redundant: since `is_targetable()` now reflects live visibility for every unit, mortars need no special-case re-check any more — `_pick_target` is back to its simpler form (SQUAD gets its own range+LOS gate; MORTAR relies entirely on the target's live is_visible).

**Verified:** a target forced visible in close contact, then moved far out of every friendly's detection range — `is_visible` correctly flips false and `is_targetable()` follows. Moved back into contact — visibility is correctly re-acquired via the normal probabilistic roll (confirmed on a real trial, ~0.5s to reacquire; noted for the record that a 3-second test window is too short to reliably catch a ~15%/sec chance and produced one false negative before widening the window — a test-timing mistake, not a game bug). Re-ran a full battle end-to-end with no runtime errors.

### 2026-09-04 — v9: enemy stopped firing while supposedly fleeing to cover

The "seek cover on first hit" mechanic (several rounds back) was working exactly as coded — `Unit.take_hit()` really did redirect an enemy squad's movement to the nearest cover the instant it took its first hit. But `BattleManager._tick_fire` never checked whether a unit was mid-relocation, only whether it was ACTIVE — so a unit "fleeing" to cover kept trading fire the entire way there. Mechanically it was heading for cover; visually and functionally it looked identical to just standing and fighting, because it never stopped shooting. This was the actual cause of "they don't immediately head for cover."

Fix: `_tick_fire` now also returns early when `unit.has_move_target` is true — any unit currently moving with intent (the initial road march, diving for cover, bolting under mortar fire) holds fire until it arrives. This applies uniformly, not just to the cover-seeking case, which is consistent: you're not in a firing position while you're still moving to one.

**Verified directly:** an enemy squad forced through its first hit — confirmed `seek_cover()` fires immediately (unchanged, was already correct) — then confirmed zero fire flashes generated while `has_move_target` was true despite a valid, in-range, spotted target sitting right next to it, and confirmed fire resumed normally the instant it arrived (`has_move_target` false). Re-ran a full battle end-to-end with no runtime errors; enemy casualties were markedly lower in that run (8%) than typical prior runs, consistent with enemies now genuinely disengaging instead of trading fire while exposed — a real balance shift worth being aware of, not a bug.

### 2026-09-04 — v10: auto-end a genuinely stalemated battle

Previously a dead battle (both mortars gone, everyone dug into cover with no LOS to anyone) just ran out the full 300-second clock, or required the player to manually order a general retreat to end it early. Added stalemate detection: `BattleManager` tracks seconds since the last shot fired (reset on any actual shot, either side) and checks each tick whether anyone is currently trying to move (mid-retreat, on the road march, or diving for cover). If 15 straight seconds pass with no shots fired AND nobody moving, the battle ends on its own — logged as "Battle stalemated" before the usual AAR report.

**Verified:** a deliberately scattered, no-contact scenario ended at t=23s (some of that beyond the 15s timeout because a couple of long-range spot rolls briefly reset the timer before contact broke again for good — the mechanism correctly treats that as "something happened," not stagnation) — versus running the full 300s previously. A normal, actively-fought battle was confirmed NOT to trip the stagnation check prematurely.

### 2026-09-04 — v11: mortar-vs-movement predictability, and a side-wide "enemy alerted" reaction

1. **Mortar fire vs. moving targets now depends on predictability.** Previously ANY moving target (squad or mortar fire, either side) got the same treatment: no cover, and an across-the-board 1.6x easier-to-hit multiplier. That's still correct for direct (squad) fire, but wrong for mortars — indirect fire has to be aimed at where the target *will be*, which only works against a steady, predictable path. Added `Unit.movement_predictable`, true only for the enemy's initial road march (a known, steady route) and false the instant anything reactive happens — `seek_cover()` and `order_retreat()` both clear it immediately. `CombatResolver.resolve_fire` now gives mortars a modest bonus against predictable movement (`MORTAR_PREDICTABLE_MOVING_MULTIPLIER` = 1.1) and a real penalty against unpredictable movement (`MORTAR_UNPREDICTABLE_MOVING_MULTIPLIER` = 0.25) — squad fire is unaffected, still the flat 1.6x either way. Verified directly (with per-trial state reset after discovering the first test run was contaminated by the existing "bolt under mortar fire" mechanic changing the condition mid-run): 46.0% hit rate against predictable movement vs. 9.8% against unpredictable, over 2000 trials each — a ~4.7x swing, matching the intended math.
2. **Coming under fire alerts the whole enemy side.** Previously only the specific squad that was shot at (or hit) reacted by breaking for cover — the rest kept marching obliviously even after a sibling squad was engaged. Added `BattleManager.enemy_alerted` (one-shot per battle): the instant any enemy squad comes under fire (shot at, hit or miss — checked right where `_tick_fire` resolves a shot against an enemy squad target), every other enemy squad still on its road march immediately breaks for cover too, exactly as if it had personally been fired on. Verified directly: one squad taking a single shot correctly flipped all six squads to `sought_cover=true`, `has_move_target` pointing at a cover position, and `movement_predictable=false` in the same tick.

**Verified before handoff:** both mechanics via targeted headless tests with real statistical sampling (not just "didn't crash"), plus a full battle run end-to-end with no runtime errors. Enemy casualties in that run were markedly low (12%) — consistent with the enemy now disengaging and taking cover far more effectively than before, on top of the last two rounds' fixes (fire-suppression while fleeing, live visibility). Worth watching for further rebalancing once actually played.

### 2026-09-04 — v12: mortars are the priority target; hills are unmistakable now

1. **Mortars are now the top-priority target for both sides.** `BattleManager._pick_target` builds its normal candidate list (respecting all existing range/LOS/visibility rules) but then checks it for any MORTAR — if one is a legal target at all, it's always chosen over a squad or the spotter, for either a squad's direct fire or another mortar's own targeting. This is on top of (not a replacement for) the existing dedicated mortar-vs-mortar counter-battery mechanic. Verified: 500/500 trials picked the mortar when one was a legal candidate; confirmed squads remain pickable when no mortar is present.
2. **Hills are now hard to miss.** Previous rendering (a faint tint + 3 subtle inset outlines) was apparently still not obvious enough on a second look. Rebuilt `GameConfig._draw_hill`: a darker halo just outside the hill's footprint gives it a raised edge against the surrounding lowland, five topographic-style contour rings brighten toward the summit (much denser and more saturated than before), and an explicit "HIGH GROUND" text label removes any remaining ambiguity.

**Verified before handoff:** mortar prioritization via statistical sampling (500 trials); hill rendering verified to run without error inside a real battle tick (draw calls execute headlessly, though the actual visual read — does it look obviously like a hill now — needs eyes-on since headless mode can't screenshot).

### 2026-09-04 — v13: spotter retreats with situational awareness

Previously every unit's retreat used the same logic — head to the single nearest cover zone, no matter what. Added `GameConfig.safest_cover_point(from, known_enemy_positions)`: restricts to the nearest few cover zones (so it doesn't trek across the map for a marginal safety gain), then picks whichever of those is farthest from the nearest currently-known enemy position. `Unit.order_retreat()` now takes an optional `known_enemy_positions` array and routes the SPOTTER specifically through this smarter logic when it isn't already in cover — squads and the mortar are unaffected, still using the plain nearest-cover behavior, matching the user's request that the spotter specifically "can be smarter." `BattleManager.order_general_retreat()` supplies the positions via a new `_known_enemy_positions()` helper — only currently *live-visible* enemies count (consistent with the rest of the game's live-visibility model, not a permanent memory of everywhere the enemy was ever seen).

**Verified:** with a known enemy sitting at one of two candidate cover zones, the spotter picked the other zone 300/300 times in both directions (enemy-at-zone-1 → always zone 2, enemy-at-zone-2 → always zone 1); confirmed `order_retreat()` correctly threads this through in isolation and that squads ignore the parameter entirely. Ran a full battle with a general retreat ordered partway through — all five player units, spotter included, withdrew safely with zero casualties, no runtime errors.

**Scoping note:** this only affects which cover zone the spotter heads to first (the first retreat leg). The final dash to the map edge (second leg) is still a straight line for everyone, spotter included — genuine route-avoidance around enemies for that leg would need real pathfinding, which is out of scope for now.

### 2026-09-04 — v14: mortar can deploy in the rear, hills block LOS, counter-battery is now a delayed positional strike

1. **Mortar gets its own, larger deployment zone.** Previously it shared the squads' village-only zone. Added `GameConfig.PLAYER_MORTAR_DEPLOYMENT_ZONE` (Rect2(20,40,400,620)) spanning from the western map edge through and past the village — real mortars sit well back from the line. Shown as an orange outline on the deployment screen alongside the squads' yellow zone and the spotter's cyan zone.
2. **Hills now block line of sight, with elevation actually mattering.** `GameConfig.has_direct_los` previously only checked buildings. Added hill-blocking: a HIGH_GROUND zone blocks LOS between two points only if BOTH are lower than it — standing on the hill (or on ground at least as high) lets you see over/down its own slope in either direction; two units in the lowland on opposite sides of the same hill genuinely cannot see each other. This affects spotting and squad direct fire; mortars remain LOS-independent as always. Verified: two lowland points on opposite sides of the hill — blocked; from atop the hill looking out past its own footprint — clear; unrelated points elsewhere — clear.
3. **Counter-battery fire is now a delayed, position-based strike instead of an instant probability check.** The trigger roll (unchanged: `MORTAR_COUNTER_BATTERY_HOLD_CHANCE`/`..._SCOOT_CHANCE`) now schedules a strike (`BattleManager._pending_counter_battery`) at the mortar's exact firing position, landing `COUNTER_BATTERY_DELAY` (3s) later. When it lands, the mortar is only at risk if it's still within `COUNTER_BATTERY_BLAST_RADIUS` (60px) of that original spot — hit chance falls off linearly with distance, reaching zero at the radius. A hold-position mortar never moves, so it's always caught square in the blast; a shoot-and-scoot mortar has almost always relocated well clear by the time the shell lands, and even if it's still nearby the odds are reduced, not eliminated — exactly "the enemy can only fire at where the mortar was when it fired." Added `log_counter_battery_incoming` and `log_counter_battery_miss` for the two new distinguishable moments (strike called in vs. strike landing without effect).

**Verified before handoff:** all three via targeted headless tests — hill LOS-blocking in three configurations; the mortar zone confirmed larger and further west than the squad zone; counter-battery confirmed non-instant (target undamaged immediately after the trigger), confirmed a mortar that stays put gets hit when the delayed strike lands, and confirmed one that relocates far away by then takes no damage. Also ran a full battle with the mortar deployed deep in the rear (50, 500) — no runtime errors, both enemy mortars destroyed.
