## Problem Statement
A for-fun side project: a single-player tactical wargame where the player commands infantry and artillery through pre-battle doctrine rather than direct real-time unit control. The enemy is AI-controlled. Drones (recon / artillery spotting) are planned for a later phase, deferred until the core loop is proven.

## What Makes This Cool
The player never issues per-unit move orders during battle. Instead, they set standing doctrine before the fight (retreat thresholds, "shoot and scoot" vs. accept counter-battery risk, initial dispositions), and units interpret that doctrine autonomously mid-fight. This sits between full RTS micromanagement and Combat Mission's WEGO order-then-watch model — closer to real mission command (auftragstaktik) than existing wargames implement. Drones later become the recon/targeting layer that makes delegated decisions consequential.

## Constraints
- Single-player only; enemy is AI-controlled (not multiplayer, not co-op).
- No map/scenario polish, no drones, no armor/air in the first build — core combat loop only (movement, line-of-sight, artillery timing/accuracy, morale/retreat).
- For-fun side project — no store/distribution pipeline needed yet.
- Battle end condition: one side fully routed (all surviving squads have crossed their own retreat threshold — simplified to a status flag, not a requirement to actually path to the map edge) or destroyed, or a fixed time limit (e.g. 5 minutes) elapses — whichever comes first. If the time limit is reached with neither side routed/destroyed, the battle ends in a draw.
- Minimal terrain: a handful of static rectangular obstacles on the map that block LOS raycasts and confer a cover bonus (reduced hit chance) to units behind them. No destructible terrain, no elevation.
- Minimal casualty model: each squad is a fixed number of pips (e.g. 4 per infantry squad, 1 per mortar crew); each resolved hit removes one pip. The "casualties exceeded 30% threshold" doctrine check is evaluated against pips lost, not a continuous stat.
- Suppression is explicitly deferred, not built into v1 (Codex's recommendation named it, but it's cut here along with ballistics dispersion and continuous attrition — see Approach A's Feasibility note). Morale/retreat in v1 is driven purely by pip loss vs. threshold, not a separate suppression stat.

## Premises
1. The doctrine-delegation mechanic (standing orders + risk tolerance, not direct unit control) is the real differentiator — more than drones.
2. A minimal slice (movement + LOS + artillery timing/accuracy, no map polish, no drones) is enough to test whether that mechanic is fun.
3. If the core loop isn't fun without drones, drones won't fix it — so drones stay deferred until the core loop is proven.
4. This is a for-fun side project — no distribution/store planning needed yet, just something playable to show wargaming hobbyists eventually.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Minimal Godot prototype (chosen)
Custom-built in Godot 4: two infantry squads + one mortar per side, a fixed-behavior scripted AI enemy (not a mirrored doctrine engine — see Next Steps), LOS raycasts against static obstacles, pip-based morale/retreat logic, doctrine panel. No map polish, no drones.
- Effort: M. Risk: Low-Medium after the v1 cuts below (the original uncut scope — full ballistics, continuous attrition, generative log — was underestimated at S/M and higher-risk; see Feasibility note).
- Pros: full control over the bespoke doctrine mechanic; produces something visual and playable to show wargaming hobbyists.
- Cons: enemy AI is a simple scripted first pass, not deeply adaptive.
- Feasibility note: the first review pass flagged that movement + LOS + morale/suppression + a doctrine engine + artillery ballistics + a decision-explaining log is more than a literal weekend at full fidelity. v1 cuts further: fixed mortar reload/relocate cooldown instead of real ballistics dispersion, pip-based squad casualties instead of continuous attrition, and a templated (not generative) combat log. Richer versions are fast-follow iteration after the core loop is validated once.

### Approach B: Fork OpenRA (ruled out for now)
Mod the open-source OpenRA RTS engine: replace click-to-command with a doctrine/intent layer, inherit its combat sim, pathfinding, and fog-of-war.
- Effort: L/XL. Risk: Medium-High.
- Ruled out because OpenRA's whole interaction model (direct unit orders) fights the exact mechanic that differentiates this game — disproportionate effort for a side project's first slice.

### Approach C: Text-log spike (ruled out for now)
Headless tick-based simulation, no graphics — resolves doctrine into outcomes and prints a combat log.
- Effort: XS/S. Risk: Very low.
- Ruled out because the user chose a small custom game with real graphics instead — wants something visual and playable, not just a numeric spike.

## Recommended Approach
Approach A: Minimal Godot prototype. It directly tests the core doctrine-delegation loop with real visual/spatial feel, is achievable in about a weekend, and produces something that can actually be demoed to wargaming hobbyists later.

## Success Criteria
Primary (subjective): after watching a squad retreat or hold under fire, does the player immediately want to revise the doctrine they gave it? If yes, the core mechanic has proven itself, independent of drones or visual polish.
Secondary (concrete): across at least 3 solo playthroughs, the player changes at least one doctrine setting between playthroughs in at least 2 of the 3 — a countable signal that the mechanic is generating real decisions, not just being watched passively.

## Combat Resolution — Minimal Spec
- Movement/engagement: squads and mortars are stationary at their deployed position for the whole battle (aside from a threshold-triggered retreat, per the Constraints' status-flag rule) and auto-fire at any enemy unit within LOS. No player-directed movement, no advance-toward-enemy behavior in v1.
- Hit chance: each firing unit has a fixed base hit probability per shot (e.g. infantry 20%, mortar 40% against a target it has LOS to — "spotted" means the same LOS raycast check used for infantry, no separate indirect-fire spotting system in v1), modified by a flat cover multiplier (e.g. x0.5) if the LOS raycast to the target crosses a static obstacle. No range falloff or line-of-fire cone in v1 — a shot either has LOS to its target or it doesn't.
- Mortar "shoot and scoot": reload is a fixed constant (e.g. 3s) separate from the player-set relocate-cooldown value. While in shoot-and-scoot mode, a mortar MUST relocate after every shot fired — it cannot fire again until relocate-cooldown + reload completes. While in "hold position" mode, a mortar skips relocation and can fire again as soon as reload alone completes, but each shot fired from a stationary position adds to a rising counter-battery chance: after firing without relocating for longer than a threshold (e.g. 2 consecutive shots), the enemy AI gains a flat 5%-per-1-second-tick chance to target that mortar's last-known firing position with its own artillery. This is the concrete risk/reward the doctrine toggle trades off.

## Doctrine Panel & Combat Log — Minimal Spec
- Doctrine panel: a simple pre-battle screen with numeric fields / sliders per squad — retreat-at-pip-loss threshold and a deploy-zone pick (one of a few pre-marked starting positions on the map, not free placement) — and per mortar — the same deploy-zone pick, plus a "hold position" vs. "shoot and scoot" toggle and the relocate-cooldown value. Edited only before the battle starts; no in-battle doctrine changes in v1.
- Combat log: templated, event-triggered text (string interpolation on trigger conditions, e.g. `"{squad} withdrew: {pips_lost}/{pips_total} casualties exceeded {threshold}"`) — not generative/NLG. Scrubbable list, newest entry at top.

## Revision Log

### 2026-09-04 — v1 implementation feedback, six changes

After the first playable prototype (Godot 4, matching Next Steps above), user feedback drove a substantial rework, implemented directly rather than through a full office-hours pass:

1. **Terrain & elevation.** Replaced the flat 2-obstacle "cover only" map with typed terrain zones (`GameConfig.TERRAIN_ZONES`): a village (BUILDING) sitting on a hill (HIGH_GROUND) that the player holds, plus TREES zones in the open ground the enemy crosses. Elevation (0 = lowland, 1 = high ground) extends detection range for whoever holds it (+150px). BUILDING/TREES now separately affect concealment (spot chance) and cover (hit chance once spotted).
2. **Spotting.** Units are invisible to the opposing side (`Unit.is_spotted`) until detected. Detection is a per-tick probability roll (`CombatResolver.roll_spot`) based on range, elevation, terrain concealment, and whether the target is moving. Only spotted units can be fired upon (`Unit.is_targetable()`). No "losing" a spot once gained — deliberate simplification to keep state legible.
3. **Enemy strength increased.** Enemy is now 3 squads + 1 mortar vs. the player's 2 squads + 1 mortar — numeric superiority via unit count, not per-unit stat buffs, for legibility.
4. **After-action report, not win/lose.** Battle end now computes held-or-not (did the player avoid being fully routed) and an exchange ratio (enemy pips lost ÷ player pips lost), producing one of four verdicts: SUCCESSFUL DEFENSE, PYRRHIC DEFENSE, COSTLY WITHDRAWAL (favorable exchange even without holding), or DEFEAT. Thresholds (1.5:1 for a clean defense, 2.0:1 for a favorable withdrawal) are a first guess, not validated against actual play.
5. **Mortar model corrected.** Removed the mortar's retreat-threshold doctrine control entirely — a mortar crew (1 pip) is either in action or knocked out by a single hit, never worn down by percent casualties the way a squad is. A knocked-out mortar gets a flavor-only crew-casualty percentage (25-100%, random) for the AAR report; it has no mechanical effect.
6. **Defense advantages via movement + firing-reveals-position.** Enemy squads now physically advance from the map edge toward the village (straight-line, no pathfinding) until reaching engagement range, then hold or retreat; the enemy mortar stays put. Movement sets `Unit.Activity.MOVING`, tripling spot chance (`GameConfig.MOVING_SPOT_MULTIPLIER`). Firing a shot unconditionally reveals the firer's position (`is_spotted = true`) regardless of terrain, on both sides symmetrically.

**Verified before handoff:** a headless Godot simulation harness (written for verification, not shipped) ran the full battle loop end-to-end across multiple random seeds with no runtime errors, producing sensible and varied outcomes (holds, defeats, and a favorable-exchange withdrawal all observed).

**Not yet tuned:** all new numeric constants (detection range, spot chance, elevation bonus, enemy advance speed, AAR verdict thresholds) are first-pass estimates. Expect to adjust them after actually playing it.

### 2026-09-04 — v2 implementation feedback, six more changes

1. **Believable map.** Terrain now renders as an actual map instead of flat tinted rectangles: hills get contour-ring shading, the village gets individual building shapes with roofs, forests get a scattered tree-dot pattern, and a road cuts across the open ground (currently decorative only — no movement bonus yet). Shared between the battle view and the deployment screen via `GameConfig.draw_terrain()` so they always match.
2. **Drag-and-drop deployment.** Replaced the zone dropdown with `DeploymentScreen` — a real map view where you drag each of your 3 squads + mortar to a starting position inside `GameConfig.PLAYER_DEPLOYMENT_ZONE` (outlined in yellow) before the battle starts. Doctrine sliders (retreat threshold, mortar shoot-and-scoot) moved to a sidebar; `main.gd` combines both into the doctrine dict on Start Battle.
3. **General retreat order.** Added `BattleManager.order_general_retreat()`, wired to an "Order General Retreat" button visible throughout the battle. Forces every ACTIVE player unit into `Unit.State.RETREATING` immediately, regardless of casualties. Added a fourth unit state, `WITHDRAWN`, reached when a retreating unit (either side) physically reaches its own safe line — only then does it stop counting as "still fighting." Battle end now waits for all retreats to actually finish before generating the report.
4. **Stronger, sharper defense advantage.** A unit caught `MOVING` now loses its terrain cover entirely and takes fire at 1.6x the normal chance (`GameConfig.MOVING_HIT_MULTIPLIER`) — moving is genuinely dangerous, not just easier to spot. Enemy squads also get a two-tier casualty reaction: past a 25% casualty threshold they log "reports heavy resistance" (flavor only), past 50% they actually retreat.
5. **Fire log removed.** No more per-shot hit/miss entries. The log now only records spotting, revealing-by-fire, threshold/ordered retreats, reaching safety, going out of action, and counter-battery hits.
6. **Force ratio widened to 3 v 6.** Player: 3 squads + 1 mortar (was 2). Enemy: 6 squads + 1 mortar (was 3) — deliberately long odds. "Held" is now judged the instant no player unit remains ACTIVE (an ordered retreat gives up the position immediately, even before the withdrawal physically completes), while the AAR's exchange-ratio verdict tiers (unchanged from the v1 revision) already cover "retreated clean after inflicting casualties" as a TACTICAL WITHDRAWAL, separate from DEFEAT.

**Verified before handoff:** re-ran the headless simulation harness (written for verification, not shipped) across two scenarios — a full fight with no manual retreat, and one with a general retreat ordered mid-battle — confirming no runtime errors, correct WITHDRAWN transitions, casualties taken during withdrawal, and the concern-threshold flavor log firing correctly.

**Not yet verified:** the actual drag-and-drop interaction (mouse-down/motion/up on the deployment screen) — headless automation doesn't simulate real input well, so this needs eyes-on testing. All new constants (moving-hit multiplier, concern threshold, retreat speeds/safe lines, deployment zone bounds) are first-pass estimates.

### 2026-09-04 — v3 implementation feedback, cover/mortar/movement rework

1. **More scattered cover.** Added two more small tree clumps flanking the road and two small isolated wall/ruin cover spots in the open ground, alongside the existing village and forest zones — the enemy now has real intermediate cover to fall back on, not just the village itself.
2. **Defenders hit harder from the start.** Player squads now fire at 0.32 base hit chance vs. the enemy's 0.20 — prepared, pre-ranged defensive positions land their first shots far more often. Mortars unaffected (indirect fire competency isn't a defense/offense asymmetry).
3. **Road march, then disperse.** Enemy squads now start with a shared movement-target system (`Unit.move_target`/`has_move_target`, replacing the old x-only advance) and initially march toward a shared rally point on the road. The first hit any enemy squad takes (of any kind, not just mortar) makes it break off toward the nearest cover zone permanently (`Unit.sought_cover`, one-time). Verified via a 200-trial isolated test that this fires correctly and doesn't re-trigger.
4. **Weapon-specific cover.** Split cover into `SQUAD_COVER_MULTIPLIER` (strong: 0.15 in buildings, 0.35 in trees) and `MORTAR_COVER_MULTIPLIER` (weak: 0.7 buildings, 0.85 trees) — direct fire struggles against cover, mortars punch through it. A moving target still loses cover entirely either way (unchanged from the prior round).
5. **Fire log removed** was already done last round; this round removed the routine per-shot text entirely from consideration and replaced it with a **visual tracer**: every shot (including indirect mortar fire with no LOS) draws a brief color-coded line + muzzle flash (`BattleManager._fire_flashes`) — yellow for enemy fire, cyan for player fire, fading over 0.3s. This is the "make it clear when the enemy is firing" requirement.
6. **Mortar-fire relocation.** A squad hit specifically by mortar fire (not direct fire) now has an independent 35% chance to bolt for the nearest cover (`Unit.bolted_for_cover`), regardless of overall casualties — verified via a 200-trial isolated test (72/200 ≈ 36%, matching the 35% target) once the automatic retreat-threshold priority (which correctly pre-empts this on a severe hit) was isolated out of the test.
7. **Mortars decoupled from direct-fire spotting.** A mortar's own fire no longer auto-reveals it to enemy squads the way a squad's muzzle flash does (`_tick_fire` only sets `is_spotted` for SQUAD kind) — it can still be found via the normal detection roll, just not automatically by firing. A mortar's targeting is now unrestricted by range (`_pick_target` only applies `SQUAD_ENGAGEMENT_RANGE` to SQUAD kind) — it fires on anything any friendly unit has spotted, anywhere, matching spotter-relayed indirect fire.
8. **Mortar-vs-mortar counter-battery**, replacing the old per-tick accrual system entirely: every time a mortar fires, the *opposing side's mortar(s) specifically* — not any other unit — get a chance to hit back (`GameConfig.MORTAR_COUNTER_BATTERY_HOLD_CHANCE` = 0.22 vs. `..._SCOOT_CHANCE` = 0.06). Mortars are now explicitly high-priority targets for each other, and shoot-and-scoot meaningfully lowers that risk.
9. **Enemy gets two mortars** (`GameConfig.ENEMY_MORTAR_POSITIONS`), player keeps one.
10. **Shoot-and-scoot visibly moves the mortar** — a short random hop (`GameConfig.MORTAR_SCOOT_HOP_DISTANCE` = 40px) after every shot in that mode, clamped to stay on the map.

**Bug caught and fixed during verification:** `nearest_cover_point` originally returned the exact same zone-center coordinate for every caller, so multiple squads converging on "the nearest cover" all stacked on the identical pixel. Fixed to return a randomized point within the zone instead.

**Also fixed:** one of the three default player squad deployment positions ((190, 430)) fell just outside the village's actual cover footprint (which ends at y=410) — moved to (190, 400).

**Verified before handoff:** full battle simulations (headless) plus two targeted 200-trial isolated unit tests (mortar-hit relocation chance, and the same check after a squad had already sought cover once) confirming both probability-gated behaviors fire at their configured rates with no runtime errors.

**Not yet tuned / observed but not changed:** with both mortars destroyed and both sides dug into strong cover, one test battle ran the full 5-minute time limit without resolving — a legitimate consequence of "cover should be hard to crack," not treated as a bug, but worth knowing about if battles feel like they drag once the mortars are gone.

No change needed — this was already the highest-priority reaction after retreat-threshold checks.

Verified directly: open-vs-building is now a **13x** swing in hit chance (was ~6.7x). Mortar cover multipliers got the same open-exposure penalty (1.3x) but kept their existing weak trees/building reduction (0.85/0.7) — mortars still punch through cover, unchanged from last round.

Deploys via its own token on the deployment screen, in a separate, more forward zone (`PLAYER_SPOTTER_DEPLOYMENT_ZONE`, outlined in cyan) than the squads/mortar (outlined in yellow) — reconnaissance doesn't belong in the same spot as firing positions. Player-side only this round; the enemy doesn't get one.

**Bug fixed while wiring up the spotter:** it briefly would have fired every single frame (fire_interval defaulted to 0 for it) before an explicit `kind == SPOTTER` guard was added as the first line of `_tick_fire` — caught before it ever ran, not from a failed test.

**Verified before handoff:** headless tests confirmed the 13x open-vs-building multiplier directly from the tables, confirmed the spotter never appears as a fire-flash source or otherwise fires across a 60-second simulated stretch, and ran a full battle to confirm the AAR report folds the spotter into casualty accounting without special-casing (it shows up as "Player Spotter" in the losses list like any other unit, using the default "was destroyed" wording since it isn't a mortar).

**Not yet tuned:** whether 13x is the right number, whether the spotter's range/concealment bonuses are well-calibrated, and whether giving only the player a spotter (not the enemy) is the right asymmetry — all first-pass judgment calls, easy to adjust once played.

### 2026-09-04 — v5 implementation feedback: LOS blocking, safer retreats, hidden spotter, less clustering

1. **Fixed: units firing through buildings.** Direct fire (squads) had no line-of-sight check at all — only the defender's own terrain mattered, so a squad could shoot straight through the village at anything spotted within range. Added `GameConfig.has_direct_los()` (segment-vs-building-rect intersection, reusing the same geometry the original v1 prototype had before the terrain-zone rewrite dropped it) and gated `BattleManager._pick_target` on it for SQUAD kind only — a building between attacker and target now blocks the shot outright, not just reduces its odds. Mortars are unaffected (indirect, spotter-relayed, as established). Verified directly: a shot straight through the village is blocked, a shot in open ground is clear, and firing out of your own building isn't self-blocked.
2. **Retreats now route through cover first.** `Unit.order_retreat()` checks whether the unit is currently in cover; if not, it first heads for the nearest cover zone (reusing the seek_cover system) before making the final dash to the safe line — a two-leg retreat instead of a beeline. Combined with fix #1, a unit that reaches cover mid-retreat can actually break direct-fire LOS from a pursuer, not just get a probability discount. A unit already in cover skips straight to the final leg. Verified both branches directly.
3. **Spotter deployment opened up + concealment overhauled.** The spotter can now be placed almost anywhere on the map (`PLAYER_SPOTTER_DEPLOYMENT_ZONE` widened from a 220x300 forward patch to nearly the full battlefield). Its concealment is now two different rules depending on terrain: hidden in trees or a building, the enemy's effective detection range against it collapses to 70px flat (overriding elevation bonuses entirely) — "unless they get very close." Standing in the open, it's found close to normally. Verified: 0/500 detections from 150px away while hidden, versus a real (if still low) chance from 40px away.
4. **Less bunching.** `nearest_cover_point` now picks among the 2-3 nearest cover zones with weighted randomness (55%/30%/15%) instead of always the single closest, on top of last round's within-zone randomization — verified 30 distinct coarse-bucket outcomes across 300 calls from one origin. The enemy's initial road-rally waypoints also got a much wider random spread (±60px x / ±120px y, up from a tight deterministic ±8px formula) so the six squads don't march down the road in a near-single-file line to begin with.

**Scoping note:** the LOS-blocking fix applies to firing/targeting only, not to spotting/detection — a squad still can't shoot through a building, but detection remains pure range+concealment with no geometry check. Not reported as broken, so left alone; flagging in case it should be extended for consistency.

**Verified before handoff:** targeted headless tests for all four fixes (LOS blocking in three configurations, hidden-vs-exposed spotter detection at two ranges over 500 trials each, both retreat-order branches, and cover-selection spread over 300 trials), plus one full battle run end-to-end with no runtime errors.

### 2026-09-04 — v6 implementation feedback: road march, mortar tracer visuals, spotter confirmation

1. **Fixed: enemy wasn't actually marching along the road.** Last round's fix for squad-clustering (a wide ±60/±120px random spread on the single road-rally waypoint) had an unintended side effect: many squads' waypoints landed well off the road strip (y:335-359) entirely, so they cut diagonally across open ground instead of visibly marching down the road. Replaced the single scattered waypoint with a real two-leg path (`Unit.set_path()` + a `move_queue`): enter the road near the spawn edge, then march west along it to the engagement point, with each squad's road-y gently staggered (338-356, safely inside the 335-359 strip) so all six are visually distinct without ever leaving the road. Verified directly: 600 position samples taken during the march phase, 0 found off the road strip.
2. **Fixed: fire visually "going through buildings."** This turned out to be specifically about mortar tracers: mortar fire is deliberately LOS-independent (by design, and explicitly reconfirmed by the user this round), but the tracer effect drew every shot — mortar included — as a straight line from firer to target. When a mortar hit a target hidden behind a building (which it's supposed to be able to do), the straight tracer visually cut right through the wall, reading exactly like "impossible" direct fire even though the underlying targeting was correct. Direct (squad) fire was re-verified to have zero LOS violations — the bug was purely in how mortar shots were drawn, not in targeting logic. Mortar tracers now draw as a lobbed arc (`BattleManager._draw_arc_tracer`, a quadratic Bezier curve) instead of a straight line, so indirect fire reads as indirect. Verified: 118 squad shots checked across a full battle, 0 LOS violations; mortar shots (34 observed) are unaffected by the check by design and now render as arcs.

### 2026-09-04 — v7: mortar fire now requires a LIVE observer, not just a past sighting

`Unit.is_spotted` never expires once set (deliberate, so the human player keeps seeing an enemy once revealed) — but that made it a poor gate for mortar fire specifically: a target glimpsed once and then broken contact behind a building remained shellable forever, since nothing ever re-checked whether anyone could *currently* see it.

### 2026-09-04 — v8: visibility is now fully live, not a permanent flag (supersedes v7's mortar-only fix)

User correction on v7: the fix there only gated *mortar targeting* against stale sightings via a dedicated check — the underlying `is_spotted` flag itself was still permanent (a design decision from several rounds back, explicitly documented as deliberate at the time: "no losing a spot once gained, to keep this legible"). The user overruled that: a unit should be visible or not visible at a given moment, full stop, for everyone and everything — not just for mortar targeting purposes.

Removed `Unit.is_spotted` entirely, replaced with `Unit.is_visible` — a fact recomputed live every tick, not a latch. `BattleManager._refresh_visibility` (replacing `_spot_side`) now does two things each tick instead of one: a target already visible stays visible only as long as `CombatResolver.has_live_observer` still holds (hard cutoff, no roll — the moment nobody has live line of sight, it goes invisible again); a target not currently visible gets the existing probabilistic `roll_spot` chance to be freshly noticed. Firing still instantly reveals a squad (unchanged), but that too is now subject to being lost again on the very next tick if no enemy unit is actually in range/LOS to have seen the muzzle flash.

Extracted `CombatResolver.effective_detection_range()` so `roll_spot` (gaining visibility) and `has_live_observer` (keeping it) use identical range rules — including the spotter's hidden-in-cover override — so the two can never disagree about how far someone can be seen.

**Verified:** a target forced visible in close contact, then moved far out of every friendly's detection range — `is_visible` correctly flips false and `is_targetable()` follows. ### 2026-09-04 — v9: enemy stopped firing while supposedly fleeing to cover

### 2026-09-04 — v10: auto-end a genuinely stalemated battle

### 2026-09-04 — v11: mortar-vs-movement predictability, and a side-wide "enemy alerted" reaction

### 2026-09-04 — v13: spotter retreats with situational awareness

### 2026-09-04 — v14: mortar can deploy in the rear, hills block LOS, counter-battery is now a delayed positional strike

1. **Mortar gets its own, larger deployment zone.** Previously it shared the squads' village-only zone. Shown as an orange outline on the deployment screen alongside the squads' yellow zone and the spotter's cyan zone.

Added hill-blocking: a HIGH_GROUND zone blocks LOS between two points only if BOTH are lower than it — standing on the hill (or on ground at least as high) lets you see over/down its own slope in either direction; two units in the lowland on opposite sides of the same hill genuinely cannot see each other. ### 2026-09-04 — v15: fixed retreat briefly moving toward the front

Bug: the "retreat via cover" first leg (added several rounds back) picked cover purely by proximity to the unit's current position, with no regard for direction. This was invisible while the mortar was confined to the village, but became a real bug once the mortar could deploy far to the rear (v14): if the nearest cover zone happened to be back toward the village — i.e., toward the enemy from the mortar's rear position — a retreat would send it there FIRST, visibly moving forward before ever heading to safety.

**Verified:** a mortar deployed deep in the rear, in the open, with every cover zone on the map to its east — `order_retreat()` correctly returned its own position as the "cover" target (i.e., no detour) rather than sending it toward any of them. ### 2026-09-05 — v16: mortar can't fire from or enter buildings; crew casualties are now an exact headcount

The mortar is now modeled as a small crew-served weapon — `Unit.MORTAR_CREW_SIZE` (4, including the driver) — rather than an abstract single-pip unit whose destruction was reported as a vague `randi_range(25, 100)` "estimated % crew casualties."

1. **A mortar can never fire from inside a building.** `BattleManager._tick_fire` now checks `GameConfig.is_building_at(unit.global_position)` for MORTAR units before the fire-timer countdown even starts — no overhead clearance to lob a round from indoors. It simply resumes firing the moment it's back in the open; the cooldown doesn't advance while blocked.
2. **A mortar can never be set up or take cover inside a building**, only in the open or in TREES:
   - `GameConfig.nearest_cover_point`/`safest_cover_point` gained an `avoid_buildings` parameter that excludes BUILDING zones from the candidate list entirely; `Unit.order_retreat()` passes `true` for MORTAR (false for everyone else — squads and the spotter still use buildings as cover, same as always).

### 2026-09-05 — v17: a real 5km map — true scale, contour elevation, natural terrain, mortar range cap

1. **Continuous elevation, not a flat zone.** The old single `HIGH_GROUND` rectangle (elevation 0 or 1, nothing in between) is gone. `GameConfig.HILLS` defines a handful of hills as `{center_m, radius_m, height_m}`; `elevation_m(pos)` is the sum of each hill's Gaussian contribution — real, continuous relief, in meters, everywhere on the map. One broad hill (35m) sits behind/around the village for a genuine defensive elevation advantage; three smaller ones add varied, natural relief elsewhere.

### 2026-09-05 — v18: a real tactical clock, decoupling how long you watch from how long the battle actually takes

The previous round made the map genuinely 5km wide but left movement speed as a frankly-labeled gameplay abstraction, because literal walking pace across a real map would take the better part of an hour to watch. This round removes that compromise by decoupling two different clocks instead: how much time YOU spend watching (actual, real engine seconds) from how much time has passed IN THE BATTLE (a tactical clock, displayed to the player, starting at 0600 for the assault). Movement, mortar flight time, and counter-battery response can now all be genuinely realistic on the tactical clock while still resolving in a couple of actual minutes to actually play.

1. **Two clocks.** `BattleManager.elapsed_time` (unchanged) is real engine seconds — it still drives the fire-tracer fade and the stagnation safeguard, both about what the player is experiencing, not the fiction. `BattleManager.scenario_elapsed_time` is new: the tactical clock, advancing every tick by `delta * time_scale`. `clock_string()` renders it as HH:MM:SS from 0600 (`GameConfig.SCENARIO_START_HOUR`), shown live in the corner of the screen (`main.gd`) in both the deployment and battle phases.
2. **Three time-scale tiers**, picked fresh every tick by `_current_time_scale()`: **fast-forward** (300x — 1 real second = 5 tactical minutes) whenever nobody on either side currently sees an enemy — no reason to sit through the ~42 tactical minutes of road march before first contact in anything but a blink; **retreat** (120x) once contact is lost but someone's still mid-retreat — still worth a glance, not worth realistic pacing; **normal** (60x — 1 real second = 1 tactical minute) the instant there's live contact — this is the "realistic and worth watching closely" pace. Live contact always wins the tier check even during a retreat (see `_current_time_scale`), so a fighting withdrawal still gets the slower, watchable pace.
3. **Movement speeds are now genuinely real**, not an abstraction: 1.4 m/s advance (a real infantry march pace), 2.0-2.2 m/s retreat, 1.8 m/s repositioning — all measured against the tactical clock, converted to on-screen pixels-per-real-second by whatever tier is active. `BATTLE_TIME_LIMIT` moved from a flat 300 (real) seconds to 14400 tactical seconds (4 tactical hours) — real infantry engagements can run long, and a realistic-paced firefight (units breaking for cover at a realistic pace, not snapping back into the fight) needs real tactical room to actually develop rather than timing out early with both sides barely scratched; even the worst case still caps actual watching time at 240 real seconds.
4. **Mortar fire is no longer instant.** A shell now takes `GameConfig.MORTAR_FLIGHT_TIME` (40 tactical seconds) to land — `BattleManager._launch_mortar_shot` schedules the impact instead of resolving it immediately, mirroring how counter-battery already worked. The mortar aims at the target's ANTICIPATED position: if the target is on a steady, predictable course (the enemy's road march), its current velocity is extrapolated across the whole flight time — a genuine lead shot; anything else (stationary or moving erratically) can only be aimed at its current position. When the shell actually lands (`_resolve_pending_mortar_shots`), if the target has since drifted more than `MORTAR_EVASION_RADIUS` (40m) from that anticipated spot — changed course, broken for cover mid-flight, kept fleeing — the round lands on empty ground: an automatic miss, no roll needed, logged distinctly (`log_mortar_shot_evaded`). A target still nearby gets the normal hit-chance roll using ITS STATE AT IMPACT. Enemy-alert and hit-consequence logging (destroyed/retreat messages) also now happen at impact, not at launch — the target doesn't know it's been fired on until the shell actually arrives.
5. **Counter-battery response time is now random, not fixed**: 1-3 tactical minutes (`COUNTER_BATTERY_DELAY_MIN`/`MAX`, replacing the old flat 3 real seconds), still triggered at the moment of firing (real counter-battery radar tracks the outgoing round, not its impact) but landing at an unpredictable interval after.
6. The AAR's "Time elapsed" line now reports the tactical duration (e.g. "2h 17m (0600 to 08:17)") instead of raw real seconds — a much more natural read for a report about a battle, not a play session.

**Verified:** headless tests confirmed `clock_string()`'s formatting and advance, the time-scale tier correctly reflecting live retreat state, a mortar shot resolving only after its full flight time (not before), a stationary target always remaining reachable after that delay, a target that flees far from the anticipated aim point reliably evading, and the counter-battery delay landing randomly within its 1-3 minute range across 200 trials. A first pass at `BATTLE_TIME_LIMIT` (90 tactical minutes) turned out too tight — full battles were consistently timing out with both sides barely scratched, since realistic movement speed means a meaningful chunk of the tactical clock now goes to marching and cover-seeking detours rather than active fighting; caught by running the same full-battle test repeatedly and noticing the identical, suspicious "resolved at exactly the time limit" result — raising it to 4 tactical hours (plus adding the fast-forward tier for the no-contact march itself) fixed it. ### 2026-09-05 — v19: the enemy commander can call off the attack, and a general retreat now has its own (faster) tactical-clock tier

Two related additions: the enemy side finally has something analogous to the player's own general-retreat button, and the tactical-clock speed-up introduced last round gets a dedicated tier for exactly that situation.

1. **`BattleManager._check_enemy_commander_retreat()`**, checked every tick: once the enemy's OVERALL casualties (summed across every enemy squad and mortar — `_enemy_situation_hopeless()`, reusing the same `_compute_side_stats` the AAR verdict already relies on) reach `GameConfig.ENEMY_COMMANDER_RETREAT_THRESHOLD` (40%), the enemy commander orders every still-ACTIVE enemy unit to retreat, one-shot per battle, logged the same way the player's own general retreat is (`log_ordered_retreat` per unit, plus a "--- Enemy commander orders a general retreat ---" banner line). This is deliberately a TOP-DOWN, whole-force judgment, distinct from `Unit._check_retreat()`'s existing bottom-up per-squad threshold (`ENEMY_RETREAT_THRESHOLD`, 50%, that unit's own casualties only) — a squad personally still at full strength can still get pulled back here if the attack as a whole has clearly failed elsewhere. Set below the per-squad threshold on purpose: by the time several individual squads have themselves crossed 50% and broken off, the whole-force aggregate is usually still well under that, so a commander-level threshold at the SAME 50% would rarely ever fire in practice — 40% was chosen empirically (see Verified below) as a level that actually triggers under decisive, but not full-wipeout, losses.
2. **A new, dedicated time-scale tier**: `GameConfig.TIME_SCALE_GENERAL_RETREAT` (180x, was previously a 120x tier keyed off "any unit retreating" — see v18). It now specifically means a GENERAL withdrawal is under way, ordered by either the player (`order_general_retreat`, now sets `player_general_retreat_ordered`) or the enemy commander (`enemy_general_retreat_ordered`, above) — not just some isolated unit routing on its own threshold while the rest of the battle carries on elsewhere, which now falls under the fast-forward tier instead (no live contact = nothing worth watching realistically, whether that's the pre-contact road march or one squad quietly walking home alone). Live contact still always wins the tier check regardless of a general retreat being under way — a fighting withdrawal stays at the realistic, watchable pace.

**Verified:** headless tests confirmed the commander orders a retreat (pulling back even an untouched squad) once aggregate casualties cross the threshold, does NOT fire while the force is still in reasonable shape, and that the tactical clock correctly reads 180x during a general withdrawal from either side but drops back to normal (60x) the instant there's live contact even mid-withdrawal. A 12-battle stress run at the initially-chosen 55% threshold never triggered the mechanic once (individual squad-level retreats at their own 50% threshold don't move the whole-force aggregate anywhere near that high in practice) despite 2/12 battles grinding all the way to the time limit — lowering the threshold to 40% got it firing under genuinely decisive losses without noticeably changing early-game behavior. All 12 re-run trials completed normally with no mortar ever ending up inside a building.

### 2026-09-05 — v22: mortars now actively hunt and hide, and the dashboard shows their status directly

Two new pieces of mortar AI, following naturally from v17/v21's range mechanics:

1. **The enemy actively closes distance to get counter-battery range on the friendly mortar.** Since counter-battery now requires real range (v21), a player mortar dug in far enough to the rear could otherwise sit permanently immune. `BattleManager._update_enemy_mortar_positioning()` checks each tick: if the friendly mortar is currently visible and beyond `MORTAR_MAX_RANGE`, an idle enemy mortar walks toward it — to just inside range (90% of max, not all the way to it), nudging its approach angle if the direct line would cut through a building. Only reacts to a CURRENTLY visible friendly mortar, matching the game's live-visibility model — no memory of where it used to be.
2. **The friendly mortar actively seeks concealment.** `GameConfig.nearest_hidden_point(from, threats, avoid_buildings)` searches expanding rings around a position for one with NO direct line of sight from any given threat — genuine concealment like the reverse slope of a hill, not just the reduced spot-CHANCE trees/buildings give as ordinary cover. The friendly mortar relocates there on exactly two triggers: right after it fires (for a hold-position mortar specifically — a shoot-and-scoot mortar already has its own separate post-fire hop, so this doesn't double up with that), and the instant it's actually spotted while sitting idle between shots. Independent of shoot-and-scoot doctrine; this is a standing survival instinct.
3. **`CasualtyDashboard` now shows mortar status explicitly.** A mortar's casualty model (an exact crew headcount, abandon-the-gun-on-any-hit) was otherwise invisible in the aggregate pip bar — one hit reads as "1 pip" there whether the whole crew died or one survivor fled. Each side's panel now has a dedicated line per mortar: "in action," "abandoned, crew fleeing (n/4 crew killed)," "withdrew safely," or "destroyed (n/4 crew killed)."

The first pass at the concealment behavior ran the "spotted" check unconditionally every tick a mortar was visible and stationary, which could preempt a ready shot the instant the mortar was spotted, and risked the mortar bouncing between spots faster than it could complete a reload if re-spotted quickly after relocating. Caught this via a stress-test timeout-rate spike (3/6 full battles hitting the exact 4-hour time limit vs. a historical ~15-25% baseline) before the user flagged that the design itself — a cooldown timer papering over the symptom — didn't match the simple rule they'd actually asked for ("relocate after firing, or when spotted"). Fixed properly by moving the "spotted between shots" check to run AFTER the fire loop each tick (so a ready shot always fires first) and adding an explicit "relocate after firing" trigger for hold-position mortars specifically, dropping the cooldown entirely — two clean, event-driven triggers instead of a continuous per-tick check plus a bolted-on timer.

**Verified:** headless tests confirmed `nearest_hidden_point` finds a genuinely LOS-blocked spot near a hill (and doesn't move when there's nothing to hide from), an out-of-range enemy mortar advances toward a visible friendly one to just inside range without cutting through buildings, an in-range or unspotted friendly mortar is left alone, a spotted friendly mortar relocates to a position verified hidden from the known threat, an unspotted one stays put, and the dashboard's per-mortar status text is correct including a fleeing/abandoned mortar. After the cooldown-vs-simple-rule fix, 8 more full-battle trials came back at a 25% timeout rate (2/8) — in line with the historical baseline, confirming the simplified design isn't a regression.

### 2026-09-05 — v23: squads spread out instead of bunching (with real teeth if they don't), the enemy mortar's chase is now relentless, more tree cover

1. **Squads no longer pile into the same patch of cover.** `GameConfig.nearest_cover_point` gained an `avoid_positions` param — given other same-side units' current (or just-decided) positions, it excludes any candidate zone one of them already occupies, as long as at least one alternative still qualifies (falls back to the shared zone rather than leaving a unit with nowhere to go). Threaded through `Unit.seek_cover()` → `Unit.take_hit()` (so breaking for cover after being hit avoids wherever an ally already is) and, most visibly, `BattleManager._alert_enemy_squads()` — when the whole side breaks for cover at once (the single most likely bunching scenario), each squad additionally avoids every zone already claimed by a squad processed earlier in that SAME alert event, not just pre-existing ally positions.
2. **Bunching now has real mechanical teeth.** `BattleManager._resolve_fire_and_check_bunching()` (now the single path both squad direct fire and mortar impacts resolve through) checks, after any hit, whether another squad on the same side is within `GameConfig.BUNCHING_RADIUS` (30m) of the one just hit — if so, a 25% (`BUNCHING_SPILLOVER_CHANCE`) roll can catch that ally in the same burst/blast too, logged distinctly (`log_bunching_spillover`). This is the actual consequence the anti-clustering behavior above is protecting against, not just cosmetic spread.
3. **The enemy mortar's hunt for counter-battery range (v22) is now genuinely high priority**, not just a one-shot decision made while idle: `_update_enemy_mortar_positioning` re-aims every tick the friendly mortar is visible and out of range (not only when the enemy mortar has no move_target yet), so it continuously corrects toward the friendly mortar's actual current position instead of plodding on toward a possibly-stale point, and now stops the INSTANT it comes into range — even mid-transit — rather than finishing a walk to a farther point computed earlier.
4. **More tree cover.** Every TREES zone scaled up 1.4x per side (~2x area) around its existing center, roughly doubling total forest coverage from ~4% to ~7.7% of the map — more cover to actually use now that squads can deploy well outside the village (v20) and spread out instead of bunching (this round).

**Verified:** headless tests confirmed tree coverage now exceeds 6% of the map, `nearest_cover_point` reliably avoids an occupied zone (and still finds cover if literally every zone is marked occupied), three squads alerted together spread across at least two different cover points rather than converging on one, a tightly bunched ally can catch spillover fire while one well outside the radius never does, and the enemy mortar both stops immediately upon entering range mid-transit and continuously redirects toward a friendly mortar that relocates while still out of range. Follow-up full-battle stress runs (6 trials) came back with no crashes and a ~17% timeout rate, consistent with the established baseline — no regression from any of these changes.

### 2026-09-05 — v24: fixed a retreat that could walk a unit straight at a known enemy

Bug report: a friendly squad's retreat once headed forward, directly toward an enemy squad. Root cause: cover-seeking's existing `retreat_dir` filter (v15) only checks that a candidate zone isn't further in the wrong X-DIRECTION than tolerance — it says nothing about the candidate's actual distance to a specific known enemy position. Worse, the real gap was upstream of that: the player's own explicit General Retreat order (`order_general_retreat`) has always threaded `known_enemy_positions` through, but a unit's AUTOMATIC, threshold-triggered retreat — the far more common case, triggered every time a hit crosses that unit's own casualty threshold — went through `Unit.take_hit() -> _check_retreat() -> order_retreat()` with NO enemy-position data at all, because `Unit` itself has no view of the wider battle and nothing was threading it in from `CombatResolver.resolve_fire`, which calls `take_hit()` directly. A retreat picked with zero idea where the enemy was could pick the "nearest" qualifying cover zone even if an enemy squad happened to be standing right in or next to it.

Fixed on two levels:
1. **A real hard safety filter.** `GameConfig.nearest_cover_point` and `safest_cover_point` both gained a `known_enemy_positions` param (via a new shared `_exclude_dangerous` helper) that HARD-excludes any candidate zone within `DANGER_RADIUS` (250m) of a known enemy position — falling back to the unrestricted set only if that would leave literally nowhere to go, same graceful-fallback pattern used everywhere else in this system. Previously only the SPOTTER's `safest_cover_point` had any enemy-awareness at all (and even that only *prefers* the safest of the nearest few zones — no hard floor); ordinary squads and the mortar had none.
2. **Actually wiring known-enemy data through to where it was missing.** `Unit.take_hit()` gained a `known_enemy_positions` parameter, threaded to `_check_retreat()` and `_apply_mortar_casualties()` — the two paths that call `order_retreat()` on their own, with no player command involved. `CombatResolver.resolve_fire()` gained the same parameter, passed straight through to `take_hit()`. `BattleManager` now computes and supplies it at every place a hit gets resolved: `_resolve_fire_and_check_bunching` (ordinary squad/mortar fire, plus the bunching-spillover hit), and `_resolve_pending_counter_battery`'s direct hit. Every automatic retreat is now exactly as enemy-aware as the player's own general-retreat command already was.

**Verified:** headless tests confirmed both cover-point functions now refuse to land inside, or within `DANGER_RADIUS` of, a zone with a known enemy in it (while still falling back to *some* cover if every zone is technically "dangerous"), and — reproducing the exact reported scenario — a squad forced across its own retreat threshold with an enemy squad sitting right on its nearest cover zone retreats somewhere else entirely, never toward or next to that enemy. Confirmed the same for a mortar's fleeing crew after both an ordinary hit and a counter-battery hit specifically (the two paths that previously had zero enemy awareness). Six follow-up full-battle stress runs completed with no crashes, all resolving naturally within budget.

### 2026-09-05 — v25: mortar relocation is now an actual walk, not an abstract cooldown, and the dashboard gives mortar status equal billing

Follow-up questions from the user exposed that `relocate_cooldown` (a player-set doctrine slider, 1-15s) never represented anything real — it was a flat time penalty bolted onto `fire_timer`, disconnected from where the mortar actually went, how far, or how fast. Removed entirely. What replaces it:

1. **Relocation is now a real, simulated walk.** `BattleManager._relocate_mortar()` picks an actual destination — the nearest point with no line of sight from any known enemy (true concealment, reusing `nearest_hidden_point` from v22), or, with no known threat to hide from, simply the nearest cover (shoot-and-scoot displaces as standing procedure regardless of visibility — see below) — and walks the mortar there via the normal move_target system at a real speed (`GameConfig.MORTAR_RELOCATE_SPEED`, a hustling pace). There's no separate cooldown on top: the walk itself is what keeps the mortar from firing again (`_tick_fire`'s existing `has_move_target` gate), for exactly as long as the real distance and speed dictate — never an arbitrary flat number.
2. **Reload time is now realistic and tactical-clock-driven.** `Unit.reload_time` changed from 3 REAL/engine seconds (an arbitrary gameplay number) to 30 TACTICAL seconds (`Unit.reload_time`, driven by `scenario_delta` now, not `delta`) — a believable lay-load-fire cycle for a deliberate, spotter-corrected shot, tied to the same real-world clock as everything else introduced in v18, not a separate abstracted rate. Relocating into TREES applies `MORTAR_RELOCATE_TREES_MULTIPLIER` (0.8x) — hauling a tube and base plate through undergrowth is real work.
4. **The two doctrines now mean something mechanically distinct**, which they didn't quite before (hold-position had accidentally picked up an unconditional post-fire relocation in v22, blurring the line): shoot-and-scoot ALWAYS relocates after firing, procedurally, whether or not it's currently spotted — that's the definition of the doctrine. Hold-position only relocates reactively, if actually spotted (`_update_friendly_mortar_concealment`'s existing per-tick check, unchanged) — it stays put and reloads faster unless something actually threatens it, the real tradeoff the doctrine choice is supposed to represent.
5. **`CasualtyDashboard` gives mortar status equal visual weight**, not a smaller, dimmer afterthought: each mortar now gets its own row at the same font size as the casualty line above it, plus a colored status bar (green/orange/gray/red for active/fleeing/withdrawn/destroyed) the same size as the casualty percentage bar — sized and styled to match, not to recede.

**Verified:** headless tests confirmed reload timing is driven by the tactical clock (not real/engine time), `_relocate_mortar` walks to a genuinely LOS-blocked destination at the correct speed, an urgent (recently-shelled) relocation goes measurably farther via the wider search rings and at the faster speed while consuming the flag, relocating into an actual TREES zone applies the terrain penalty, a counter-battery strike landing nearby sets the evading flag, shoot-and-scoot always relocates after firing even with no known threats while hold-position does not unless spotted, and the dashboard renders one equally-sized, correctly color-coded row per mortar (hiding unused rows on the player's single-mortar side). ### 2026-09-05 — v27: the enemy mortar's counter-battery chase no longer requires an actual visual spot

Bug report: enemy mortars weren't trying to get into counter-battery range at all. Root cause: `_update_enemy_mortar_positioning` (v22, sharpened in v23) required the friendly mortar to be CURRENTLY visible (`is_visible`) before the enemy would react — but v22/v25 also gave the friendly mortar an aggressive concealment-seeking habit (hide immediately after every shot, or the instant it's spotted between shots). Combined, the friendly mortar had become so good at staying hidden that the enemy rarely got a large enough visibility window to ever notice it, let alone chase it — the mechanic existed but had almost nothing to trigger on in practice.

The fix models something the game already had half of: real counter-battery detection doesn't require ongoing visual contact with the crew — it comes from tracking the outgoing round's muzzle blast or trajectory the instant it fires (`_resolve_mortar_counter_battery`'s whole premise already relies on this). `_launch_mortar_shot` now records `_last_detected_mortar_fire[mortar] = {position, time}` on EVERY shot, any mortar, regardless of visibility. A new `_known_friendly_mortar_position()` gives the enemy's chase logic a position to work with from two sources — live visibility if currently available (most accurate), falling back to the last detected firing position if that's within `MORTAR_FIRE_DETECTION_EXPIRY` (180 tactical seconds, roughly the counter-battery response window) — old enough and the mortar's almost certainly moved on, not worth chasing a stale fix. A mortar that hides perfectly between shots no longer becomes un-huntable — it's still given away every time it actually fires, which is the whole point of having fired in the first place.

**Verified:** headless tests confirmed no fix exists before the mortar has ever been seen or heard firing, live visibility gives an exact fix, firing gives a fix even when the mortar was never visually spotted at all, a stale detection expires correctly, live visibility is preferred over an older detected position when both are available, and — directly reproducing the reported bug — an enemy mortar successfully chases and reaches counter-battery range using ONLY a detected firing position, having never once visually spotted the target. A dedicated full-battle run with the friendly mortar deployed deep in the rear (4662m/4700m from the two enemy mortars, both initially out of range) confirmed both enemy mortars closed distance and reached exactly-in-range positions (3495m/3496m) purely from firing detection. ### 2026-09-05 — v28: a real structural deadlock (100% of battles!) and the friendly mortar's over-eager fleeing, both fixed

Bug report: an implausibly clean "SUCCESSFUL DEFENSE" (0% player casualties, only 15% enemy casualties, "Enemy withdrew: Enemy Squad, Enemy Squad") — the user correctly judged this shouldn't have happened. Investigation found something much more severe than a bad verdict: **10 out of 10 test battles were ending via the stagnation timeout** (`_check_battle_end`'s "nobody's fired or moved in 15 real seconds" safety valve), not through actual decisive combat. Dumping full battlefield state at the moment of stagnation showed why: multiple squad pairs had completely clear LOS to each other but every one sat 500-2200m apart — well beyond `SQUAD_ENGAGEMENT_RANGE` (400m) — with nothing on either side able to close that distance. Root cause: an enemy squad that breaks for cover under fire has never had any way to resume its advance afterward — it just holds that position for the rest of the battle. That was always true, but harmless while cover was concentrated near the village (v1-era map); now that tree cover is doubled and spread across the whole 5km map (v23), a squad breaking off early in its march can land somewhere permanently out of range of everything, with no path back into the fight. The stagnation timeout was correctly detecting a real dead end — the actual bug was upstream, in the AI never trying to un-stall itself.

Fixed with `BattleManager._update_enemy_squad_advance()`: an enemy squad that has already broken for cover (`sought_cover`), is currently idle, and has nothing worth shooting at (`_pick_target` comes up empty) now resumes closing on `GameConfig.VILLAGE_CENTER` in bounded rushes (`ENEMY_ADVANCE_RUSH_DISTANCE`, 400m per leg) rather than sprinting blindly — it still pauses to reassess (and fire, if something's now in range) between rushes, real fire-and-movement rather than one long blind dash. Re-measured: full stagnation-ending rate dropped from 10/10 to roughly 30-40%, and inspecting those remaining cases individually showed they're now genuine dead ends — a single isolated player squad, physically cut off from the enemy-occupied village by a hill or building with no possible LOS, nothing left to resolve. (One earlier reading of "6/10 still stagnating" during this investigation turned out to be inflated by a bug in the TEST script itself, reusing one `CombatLog` across trials so a stale "stalemated" message from an earlier trial falsely tainted a later one that had actually ended in a real defeat — worth noting since it looked like a much smaller improvement than it actually was until caught.)

Second report, same conversation: the friendly mortar didn't need to run from counter-battery range — it could stand and fight, or simply stay concealed — and wasn't being careful enough to remain out of LOS in the first place. Three fixes:
1. **`_update_friendly_mortar_concealment` now checks for a live target first** — being spotted no longer means automatic flight; if the mortar currently has something worth shooting at (including an enemy mortar that's wandered into range), it stands and fights. Concealment is the fallback for being exposed with nothing to show for it, not a reflex to being seen.
2. **`GameConfig.nearest_hidden_point` now tries the reverse slope of an actual nearby hill first** (`_reverse_slope_candidate` — a point 75% of the way down a hill's radius, on the far side from the threats' average direction), before falling back to the previous random-ring sampling. Real elevation masking like this tends to stay valid even if a threat shifts position somewhat; a point that merely tests clear against today's exact threat positions doesn't have that property.
3. **The friendly mortar is now never visually spotted at all**, categorically (`_refresh_visibility` skips it outright, even at point-blank range with dead-clear LOS) — a well-sited, camouflaged crew isn't something rifle squads happen to notice by scanning the horizon in this game's model. The only way the enemy ever gets a fix on it is by detecting it actually firing (the muzzle-blast/trajectory detection from v27's counter-battery-range chase) — which also means the "spotted, flee" reactive trigger now essentially never fires for a hold-position mortar at all; it just holds its ground unless it draws counter-battery fire, exactly matching "should never allow the enemy to observe it directly."

**Verified:** headless tests confirmed an idle enemy squad with nothing to shoot at resumes its advance while one that still has a target does not, that the resumed advance moves in bounded legs rather than one sprint, that the friendly mortar stays invisible under a barrage of forced spotting rolls at point-blank range yet still yields a detection fix the instant it fires, that a spotted mortar with a live target stands and fights instead of fleeing, and that the reverse-slope search finds a genuinely hidden spot next to an actual hill directly rather than by chance. Six full-battle stress runs (both doctrines) and a further 15-trial stagnation-rate check afterward confirmed no crashes and a stable, now-legitimate stagnation rate.

### 2026-09-05 — v29: mortars now lead a retreating target, and a unit under fire while retreating zigzags

Bug report: "When units are retreating, the mortar shooting at them should try to lead them. I don't think it does. But if a retreating unit is taking artillery fire, it should zig zag a bit to reduce the chance of getting hit."

The user was right about the first half. `BattleManager._mortar_aim_point()` only extrapolated an aim point ahead of a target when `Unit.movement_predictable` was true — but `order_retreat()` always sets `movement_predictable = false` (it also drives a separate, deliberately-unrelated accuracy penalty in `CombatResolver.resolve_fire` for genuinely erratic movement), so a retreating unit was never led at all, only ever shot at its exact current position. Fixed by decoupling the two: `_mortar_aim_point()` now extrapolates whenever the target's heading is actually knowable right now — `has_move_target` (a retreat's first leg to cover, or any other directed move) for the direction and speed, or, new, a direct `Unit.State.RETREATING` branch using the fixed team-based retreat direction and `retreat_speed` for the unescorted final dash to safety. `movement_predictable` keeps its old, narrower job untouched: only the hit-chance multiplier, not whether a shot leads at all.

Second half: a retreating unit that has actually had a mortar round resolve against it (hit, or a near-miss still within `MORTAR_EVASION_RADIUS`) now starts juking — `Unit.zigzagging`, set in `_resolve_pending_mortar_shots` and never cleared for the rest of the withdrawal, since once you know you're under a barrage you keep broken-field running rather than relaxing after one lucky dodge. Only affects the final retreat leg (`_step_retreat` — exactly the leg the lead-aiming fix above can otherwise lead cleanly); the first leg to cover already uses ordinary point-to-point movement. Implemented as discrete random lateral "jinks" rather than a smooth sine wave — a periodic motion has a period a mortar's own extrapolation could eventually ride out, so direction and speed re-roll at a randomized interval (`ZIGZAG_JINK_MIN_INTERVAL`/`MAX_INTERVAL`, 6-12 tactical seconds) at a fraction of the unit's own retreat speed (`ZIGZAG_LATERAL_SPEED_FRACTION`, 0.6) — real sideways progress, not a wobble in place. No new hit-chance math: this works entirely through the existing drift-vs-`MORTAR_EVASION_RADIUS` check, exactly like any other reason a target might not be where a shell lands.

(Counter-battery fire (`_resolve_pending_counter_battery`) only ever resolves against a mortar still in `State.ACTIVE` — a retreating unit is unreachable by that path today, so it was left out of the zigzag trigger; nothing currently exercises it, and adding it in would be speculative.)

**Verified:** headless tests confirmed a retreating target's aim point now leads by `retreat_speed * MORTAR_FLIGHT_TIME`, that a cover-bound squad is led along its actual heading regardless of `movement_predictable`, that `zigzagging` gets set on both a resolved hit and an evaded near-miss against a retreating target (but not otherwise), that a zigzagging unit's lateral position visibly wanders over time while a non-zigzagging one holds a perfectly straight line, and an 8-trial full-battle stress run with real retreats occurring in every run confirmed no crashes or hangs with both mechanics active together.

### 2026-09-05 — v30: losing a mortar (or its crew) now counts in the AAR exactly like losing a squad

Bug report: "In evaluating the battle results, losing the mortar should count against the player, and killing enemy mortars should count in favor. Mortar crew casualties should count the same as any other casualties." — clarified this was specifically about the final battle report, not the retreat-threshold trigger logic (which was already unaffected).

Root cause: a mortar's `max_pips` was hardcoded to `1`, while its actual crew strength (`crew_size`, 4) was tracked entirely separately via `crew_killed`. `take_hit()` unconditionally dropped a unit's `pips` by 1 before branching on kind, so a mortar's single pip was always fully spent on its *first* hit — whether that hit killed one crew member (leaving three survivors who abandon the gun and retreat) or wiped the whole crew outright. Since `BattleManager._compute_side_stats()` (the exact numbers the AAR's casualty percentages and exchange ratio are built from) sums `max_pips`/`pips` across every unit on a side, a mortar's fate — grazed or annihilated — always cost the side a flat, trivial 1 pip out of the total, dwarfed by any squad's 4. Destroying an enemy mortar barely moved the exchange ratio; losing your own barely dented your casualty percentage.

Fixed by making a mortar's pips real crew accounting instead of a binary flag: `max_pips` is now set to `crew_size` in `setup()`, and `_apply_mortar_casualties()` sets `pips = crew_size - crew_killed` directly (rather than `take_hit()`'s generic `pips -= 1`, which no longer runs for mortars at all — moved below the mortar branch's `return`). A mortar that loses one crew member out of four now shows 3/4 pips, same granularity as a squad; one that loses its whole crew shows 0/4 — costing the side exactly as many pips as a fully destroyed squad, not a token 1. No other mortar behavior changes: still one random casualty roll per hit (`randi_range(1, remaining)`), still abandons the gun and retreats the instant any crew survive, still only truly `DESTROYED` when the last crew member falls.

**Verified:** headless tests confirmed a fresh mortar starts at `max_pips == crew_size` pips, a partial hit sets `pips == crew_size - crew_killed` and sends survivors into `RETREATING` rather than ending the unit, a full-crew loss zeroes pips and sets `DESTROYED`, and `_compute_side_stats` on a one-squad-one-mortar side now costs exactly the same pips (4) for destroying either one — where before the mortar cost only 1. A 6-trial full-battle stress run confirmed the new `pips_total` (17 = 3 squads × 4 + mortar's 4 + spotter's 1) held up end-to-end with no crashes.

### 2026-09-05 — v31: the AAR report text no longer overflows its background box

Bug report (screenshot): the after-action report's text was spilling out past the right edge of its dark background panel, onto the battlefield map behind it — e.g. "Enemy withdrew: ... Enemy Mortar (2/4 crew killed, g" running straight off the edge instead of wrapping.

Root cause: `main.gd`'s report `Label` was added directly as a child of the `ColorRect` background — a plain `Control`, not a `Container`. A bare `Control` never actually caps a child's width below that child's own *natural* (unwrapped) content size: `custom_minimum_size` can only raise a control's effective minimum, never lower it below what the control would otherwise need. So even with `autowrap_mode` set and an explicit `size = Vector2(540, 480)` assigned, Godot silently re-expanded the label back out to whatever width its longest unwrapped line actually needed (over 1000px for a long casualty list) the moment that exceeded 540 — autowrap never got a chance to do anything, because the label was never actually narrow enough to need to wrap. Confirmed directly: an isolated reproduction of the exact same property assignments, in the exact same order, showed the label's final size stuck at `(1070, 480)` regardless of whether `autowrap_mode` was set before or after `size` — proving this wasn't a property-ordering bug but a structural one.

Fixed by giving the label a real `Container` ancestor, matching the pattern `combat_log.gd` already uses successfully for its own scrollable text: a `ScrollContainer` (540×480, horizontal scrolling disabled so it can't just let the line run off sideways instead of wrapping) between the background and the label, with the label's `size_flags_horizontal` set to `SIZE_EXPAND_FILL`. A `Container` *does* forcibly assign its child's width from the outside, which is the one thing a bare `Control` parent won't do — so the label now actually wraps at 540px, and the `ScrollContainer` clips (and, if a report ever runs long enough, scrolls) anything that still doesn't fit vertically, rather than letting it spill out over the map.

**Verified:** reproduced the exact bug headlessly first (bare-Control parent → label stuck at its unwrapped natural width, confirmed numerically), then confirmed the `ScrollContainer` fix constrains the label to the intended 540px width and keeps its right edge inside the background panel's own right edge, using the same long multi-squad casualty-list text from the bug report.

### 2026-09-05 — v32: significantly more forest, as irregular natural blobs instead of rectangles

**Verified:** headless tests confirmed the patch count increased as intended (and `TERRAIN_ZONES` now holds only buildings), that a patch's actual radius varies meaningfully by angle and that points just past its base radius can fall genuinely outside it (i.e., this isn't secretly still a circle or square), that BUILDING still takes precedence over TREES at a shared point, that `nearest_cover_point`/`safest_cover_point` always land inside real cover terrain (including with `avoid_buildings=true`, which continues to correctly exclude every building), and that trees still don't affect `has_direct_los` (unchanged). ### 2026-09-05 — v33: a second fieldable setup — a drone team replacing the artillery spotter

Request: at launch, let the player choose between "Level 0" (the existing artillery spotter) and "Level 1," a drone team — a 3-person crew flying a rotation of 4 real Mavic-class scout drones (12km round-trip range, ~21-minute flight time each), always keeping exactly one airborne (scouting, mortars the top priority) and a second standing by fully charged in case the first is shot down, with the other two recovering/recharging. The drone loiters at 300m, "hard to see and hard to hit," and gives a genuinely better view than a ground spotter — including catching a shoot-and-scoot mortar's new position and seeing through concealment that would fool a ground observer. The user also specified, mid-implementation, the real 21-minute Mavic flight-time spec, and that a routine swap should have no coverage gap — the standby launches as the old one is coming in, not after it's actually landed.

This is the largest single change of the session — a new game mode, a new unit lifecycle, and new spotting rules — so it leaned hard on reusing existing, already-proven machinery rather than inventing parallel systems:

1. **Mode selection**: a new `LevelSelectScreen` (`GameConfig.ReconMode.SPOTTER`/`DRONE_TEAM`) is now the very first thing `main.gd` shows, before deployment. The choice persists across "try again" restarts within a session — level-select only appears once, at launch.

2. **The drone team** (`Unit.Kind.DRONE_TEAM`) is a normal deployed unit, reusing the spotter's exact deployment zone/position and — mechanically — the mortar's crew-casualty model verbatim (`_apply_crew_casualties`, renamed from `_apply_mortar_casualties` now that two kinds share it): `max_pips == crew_size` (3), one decisive hit either kills some crew and sends survivors fleeing (no more drone operations once that happens) or wipes the whole crew outright. It never fires and never itself observes anything — it's purely the fleet's home base.

3. **The airborne drone** (`Unit.Kind.DRONE`) is the one genuinely new lifecycle in the game: unlike every other unit, it's created and freed per-sortie rather than existing for the whole battle. Only the CURRENTLY airborne drone is a real `Unit`; the rest of the 4-airframe fleet is plain bookkeeping on `BattleManager` (`_drones_ready_for_launch`, `_drone_recovering` timers, `_drones_destroyed`) — a grounded drone isn't part of the battle in any way. `_update_drone_operations` runs the whole rotation every tick: ticks recovery timers, updates the airborne sortie's flight-time/range budget (`GameConfig.DRONE_MAX_FLIGHT_TIME`/`DRONE_ROUND_TRIP_RANGE`) and re-picks its search target, and — in ONE single check — launches a fresh drone the instant the sky is empty and a standby is ready. Routing both "just got shot down" (via the `state_changed` signal clearing `active_drone`) and "hit its flight budget" (`_update_active_drone` clearing it directly) through that same single launch check means a swap is never more than one tick late either way, which is what actually delivers the "no gap on a routine swap" behavior — the standby launches the same tick the old one turns for home, not after any simulated flight back.

4. **Search AI** (`_drone_search_target`, re-evaluated every tick, not set once): orbit a currently-visible enemy mortar if there is one (by far the highest priority, per the request) → failing that, fly to wherever an enemy mortar was last DETECTED FIRING (a new `_known_enemy_mortar_fire_position`, the exact mirror of the existing `_known_friendly_mortar_position` used for the enemy's own counter-battery chase — same underlying `_last_detected_mortar_fire` dict, just read from the other side) → failing that, any other visible enemy unit → with no lead at all, sweep toward the road corridor to establish contact. "Sees where a shoot-and-scoot mortar moved to" falls out of this almost for free: the drone's detection range is big enough that simply staying near a mortar's last-known area re-acquires it after a nearby relocation, no separate tracking logic needed.

5. **A genuinely better sensor**, not the spotter's numbers with a bonus bolted on: `effective_detection_range`/`roll_spot`/`has_live_observer` (`CombatResolver`) now branch on `observer.kind == DRONE` — a much larger flat detection range (no elevation-bonus double-dipping, since it's already always higher than anything on the map), `has_aerial_los` instead of `has_direct_los` (identical BUILDING blocking, but NO ground-elevation masking at all — 300m clears every hill on this 35m-max-height map), and its own concealment table where TREES is a much smaller penalty than at ground level (porous canopy from above) but BUILDING is a bigger one (a roof fully hides what's under it regardless of viewing angle) — the reverse of the ground-level ordering. This is the actual mechanism behind "may see enemies who think they're concealed."

6. **Hard to see, hard to hit**: as a TARGET, the drone skips terrain-based concealment/cover entirely (it isn't standing on any of it) in favor of two flat, severe multipliers — `DRONE_SPOT_CHANCE_MULTIPLIER` (0.15) and `DRONE_HIT_CHANCE_MULTIPLIER` (0.1) — applied regardless of who's looking or shooting. One hit still destroys it outright (`max_pips = 1`, unmanned, no crew to partially lose) but getting that hit is deliberately rare.

7. **Casualty accounting**: a drone is equipment, not personnel — the 3-person crew is safe at the ground station regardless of what happens to whatever's flying. `_compute_side_stats` now explicitly skips `Kind.DRONE` when summing `pips_total`/`pips_lost` (though a destroyed one still appears in the AAR's loss list, just not in the casualty percentage), and `_anyone_moving`/`_all_done_fighting`/`_has_active_units` all skip it too — it's ALWAYS "moving with intent" while airborne (constantly re-picking a search target), which would otherwise both defeat the stalemate timeout and let a lone flying camera falsely keep a wiped-out side "still fighting" or "holding the village."

8. **Dashboard**: a drone-fleet status row (airborne/ready/recharging-with-ETA/lost counts, or the ground crew's own status if IT'S out of action) with the same visual weight as the existing mortar row, shown only in DRONE_TEAM mode.

**Verified:** headless tests covering the full fleet-rotation lifecycle — correct initial rotation (1 airborne + 1 standby + 2 recharging), a shoot-down triggering the standby's launch on the very next tick, a routine RTB (both time-budget and range-budget triggers) doing the same with zero gap when a standby is ready, aerial LOS genuinely ignoring a hill that blocks ground-level LOS between the same two points, the search AI's priority order (visible mortar > last-detected-fire position), drone hit/spot rates landing near the configured severe multipliers over 2000 trials, drone losses correctly excluded from the human-casualty tally while drone-team crew casualties correctly feed into it exactly like a mortar's, and the drone-alone exclusion from the stalemate/holding checks. ### 2026-09-05 — v37: contour lines were crossing near the top-left of the map

Checked the gameplay/geometry model FIRST, before touching anything, since that's the more serious possibility the follow-up raised: `elevation_m()` already computes a single continuous elevation at any point on the map as the sum of every hill's (warped) Gaussian contribution — there's no separate or partial version of it — and `has_direct_los()` already samples that exact same combined function at 20 points along every sightline. That part was already correct and needed no rework; it's what the original per-hill drawing bug never actually touched.

The real bug was isolated entirely to `_draw_hills()`: each hill drew its OWN contour rings from ONLY its own Gaussian formula (`_hill_radius_warp` scaled by that one hill's own height/radius), completely ignoring every other hill's contribution to the actual combined field `elevation_m()` computes. For any hill far enough from its neighbors this is indistinguishable from correct (the other hills' contributions there really are negligible) — but the hill at (350m, 550m), "ridge west of the village, rear," sits close enough to the village hill at (1300m, 1550m) — center-to-center distance ~1380m against a combined halo reach of ~1440m — that their independently-drawn rings visibly overlapped in exactly the top-left area reported, and wherever two hills' rings cross like that, it looks exactly like "impossible" crossing contours, because they don't actually represent the one true elevation field at that spot.

Fixed by tracing REAL contour lines of the combined field instead: a standard marching-squares algorithm over a grid sampled from the actual `elevation_m()` (40m spacing), computed once and cached (`_contour_segments_cache`) since the terrain is static and never needs retracing after the first draw. `_draw_hills` now just replays those cached line segments every frame, colored by level exactly as before, rather than drawing each hill's isolated ring.

**Verified:** headless tests confirmed the cache produces a substantial number of real traced segments; that a sample of segment midpoints actually have elevation (via the real `elevation_m()`) matching their claimed contour level, both generally and specifically within the village-hill/west-ridge overlap zone that produced the original bug report; and — the actual claim under test — that no two segments from DIFFERENT contour levels intersect anywhere on the map (checked pairwise within a spatial-bucket neighborhood, not a full unrestricted crossing scan, for tractability across ~1000 segments), confirming real contour lines never cross now that they're traced from the one true field instead of approximated per-hill.

### 2026-09-05 — v38: crash — a pending mortar shot could hold a reference to a drone that had already landed

Bug report: a Godot editor screenshot showing "Trying to assign invalid previously freed instance" at `_resolve_pending_mortar_shots`, `var target: Unit = shot.target`.

Root cause: mortar fire isn't resolved instantly — a shot is recorded in `_pending_mortar_shots` at launch (holding a direct reference to its target) and only resolved `MORTAR_FLIGHT_TIME` (40 tactical seconds) later. Every unit kind except one persists in `player_units`/`enemy_units` for the whole battle even once destroyed or withdrawn (a permanent husk, which is exactly why `_resolve_pending_mortar_shots` already checked `target.state == DESTROYED/WITHDRAWN` as a normal, expected case). `Kind.DRONE` is the one exception introduced in v33/v34: a drone that completes a routine return-to-base is actually `queue_free()`'d once it lands (see `_update_returning_drone`), not left behind as a husk. If an enemy mortar targets a drone and that drone lands normally before the 40-second flight time is up — an entirely routine, expected sequence, not a rare edge case — the pending shot's `target` reference goes stale, and the existing `DESTROYED`/`WITHDRAWN` state check never even runs: reading `.state` off an actually-freed Object, or in this case just assigning it to a typed `Unit` variable at all, is itself what throws "Trying to assign invalid previously freed instance."

Fixed with an `is_instance_valid()` check — but the first attempt still crashed on the identical line, because the check ran on the ALREADY-typed `var target: Unit = shot.target` assignment, and it's that exact assignment (not anything done with `target` afterward) that raises the error for a freed instance. Moving the validity check to run on the raw `shot.target` Variant, before it's ever assigned to a typed `Unit` variable, actually fixed it.

**Verified:** wrote a direct reproduction first — a pending shot targeting a drone, the drone force-landed (freed) exactly like a routine RTB does, then the shot resolved after its flight time — which reproduced the exact reported error message and stack location before the fix, and resolved with no error after moving the check before the typed assignment. A 15-trial full-battle stress run in drone mode (long enough to include real enemy-mortar-vs-drone engagements and routine landings happening concurrently) completed with no crashes or errors.
