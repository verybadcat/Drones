## Problem Statement
A for-fun side project: a single-player tactical wargame where the player commands infantry and artillery through pre-battle doctrine rather than direct real-time unit control. The enemy is AI-controlled. Drones (recon / artillery spotting) are planned for a later phase, deferred until the core loop is proven.

## What Makes This Cool
The player never issues per-unit move orders during battle. Instead, they set standing doctrine before the fight (retreat thresholds, "shoot and scoot" vs. accept counter-battery risk, initial dispositions), and units interpret that doctrine autonomously mid-fight. This sits between full RTS micromanagement and Combat Mission's WEGO order-then-watch model — closer to real mission command (auftragstaktik) than existing wargames implement. Drones later become the recon/targeting layer that makes delegated decisions consequential.

## Constraints
- Single-player only; enemy is AI-controlled (not multiplayer, not co-op).
- No map/scenario polish, no drones, no armor/air in the first build — core combat loop only (movement, line-of-sight, artillery timing/accuracy, morale/retreat).
- For-fun side project — no store/distribution pipeline needed yet.
- Battle end condition: one side fully routed (all surviving squads have crossed their own retreat threshold — simplified to a status flag, not a requirement to actually path to the map edge) or destroyed, or a fixed time limit (e.g. 5 minutes) elapses — whichever comes first. If the time limit is reached with neither side routed/destroyed, the battle ends in a draw.
- Minimal terrain: a handful of static rectangular obstacles on the map that block LOS raycasts and confer a cover bonus (reduced hit chance) to units behind them. No destructible terrain, no elevation.
- Minimal casualty model: each squad is a fixed number of pips (e.g. 4 per infantry squad, 1 per mortar crew); each resolved hit removes one pip. The "casualties exceeded 30% threshold" doctrine check is evaluated against pips lost, not a continuous stat.
- Suppression is explicitly deferred, not built into v1 (Codex's recommendation named it, but it's cut here along with ballistics dispersion and continuous attrition — see Approach A's Feasibility note). Morale/retreat in v1 is driven purely by pip loss vs. threshold, not a separate suppression stat.

## Premises
1. The doctrine-delegation mechanic (standing orders + risk tolerance, not direct unit control) is the real differentiator — more than drones.
2. A minimal slice (movement + LOS + artillery timing/accuracy, no map polish, no drones) is enough to test whether that mechanic is fun.
3. If the core loop isn't fun without drones, drones won't fix it — so drones stay deferred until the core loop is proven.
4. This is a for-fun side project — no distribution/store planning needed yet, just something playable to show wargaming hobbyists eventually.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Minimal Godot prototype (chosen)
Custom-built in Godot 4: two infantry squads + one mortar per side, a fixed-behavior scripted AI enemy (not a mirrored doctrine engine — see Next Steps), LOS raycasts against static obstacles, pip-based morale/retreat logic, doctrine panel. No map polish, no drones.
- Effort: M. Risk: Low-Medium after the v1 cuts below (the original uncut scope — full ballistics, continuous attrition, generative log — was underestimated at S/M and higher-risk; see Feasibility note).
- Pros: full control over the bespoke doctrine mechanic; produces something visual and playable to show wargaming hobbyists.
- Cons: enemy AI is a simple scripted first pass, not deeply adaptive.
- Feasibility note: the first review pass flagged that movement + LOS + morale/suppression + a doctrine engine + artillery ballistics + a decision-explaining log is more than a literal weekend at full fidelity. v1 cuts further: fixed mortar reload/relocate cooldown instead of real ballistics dispersion, pip-based squad casualties instead of continuous attrition, and a templated (not generative) combat log. Richer versions are fast-follow iteration after the core loop is validated once.

### Approach B: Fork OpenRA (ruled out for now)
Mod the open-source OpenRA RTS engine: replace click-to-command with a doctrine/intent layer, inherit its combat sim, pathfinding, and fog-of-war.
- Effort: L/XL. Risk: Medium-High.
- Ruled out because OpenRA's whole interaction model (direct unit orders) fights the exact mechanic that differentiates this game — disproportionate effort for a side project's first slice.

### Approach C: Text-log spike (ruled out for now)
Headless tick-based simulation, no graphics — resolves doctrine into outcomes and prints a combat log.
- Effort: XS/S. Risk: Very low.
- Ruled out because the user chose a small custom game with real graphics instead — wants something visual and playable, not just a numeric spike.

## Recommended Approach
Approach A: Minimal Godot prototype. It directly tests the core doctrine-delegation loop with real visual/spatial feel, is achievable in about a weekend, and produces something that can actually be demoed to wargaming hobbyists later.

## Success Criteria
Primary (subjective): after watching a squad retreat or hold under fire, does the player immediately want to revise the doctrine they gave it? If yes, the core mechanic has proven itself, independent of drones or visual polish.
Secondary (concrete): across at least 3 solo playthroughs, the player changes at least one doctrine setting between playthroughs in at least 2 of the 3 — a countable signal that the mechanic is generating real decisions, not just being watched passively.

## Combat Resolution — Minimal Spec
- Movement/engagement: squads and mortars are stationary at their deployed position for the whole battle (aside from a threshold-triggered retreat, per the Constraints' status-flag rule) and auto-fire at any enemy unit within LOS. No player-directed movement, no advance-toward-enemy behavior in v1.
- Hit chance: each firing unit has a fixed base hit probability per shot (e.g. infantry 20%, mortar 40% against a target it has LOS to — "spotted" means the same LOS raycast check used for infantry, no separate indirect-fire spotting system in v1), modified by a flat cover multiplier (e.g. x0.5) if the LOS raycast to the target crosses a static obstacle. No range falloff or line-of-fire cone in v1 — a shot either has LOS to its target or it doesn't.
- Mortar "shoot and scoot": reload is a fixed constant (e.g. 3s) separate from the player-set relocate-cooldown value. While in shoot-and-scoot mode, a mortar MUST relocate after every shot fired — it cannot fire again until relocate-cooldown + reload completes. While in "hold position" mode, a mortar skips relocation and can fire again as soon as reload alone completes, but each shot fired from a stationary position adds to a rising counter-battery chance: after firing without relocating for longer than a threshold (e.g. 2 consecutive shots), the enemy AI gains a flat 5%-per-1-second-tick chance to target that mortar's last-known firing position with its own artillery. This is the concrete risk/reward the doctrine toggle trades off.

## Doctrine Panel & Combat Log — Minimal Spec
- Doctrine panel: a simple pre-battle screen with numeric fields / sliders per squad — retreat-at-pip-loss threshold and a deploy-zone pick (one of a few pre-marked starting positions on the map, not free placement) — and per mortar — the same deploy-zone pick, plus a "hold position" vs. "shoot and scoot" toggle and the relocate-cooldown value. Edited only before the battle starts; no in-battle doctrine changes in v1.
- Combat log: templated, event-triggered text (string interpolation on trigger conditions, e.g. `"{squad} withdrew: {pips_lost}/{pips_total} casualties exceeded {threshold}"`) — not generative/NLG. Scrubbable list, newest entry at top.

## Revision Log

### 2026-09-04 — v1 implementation feedback, six changes

After the first playable prototype (Godot 4, matching Next Steps above), user feedback drove a substantial rework, implemented directly rather than through a full office-hours pass:

1. **Terrain & elevation.** Replaced the flat 2-obstacle "cover only" map with typed terrain zones (`GameConfig.TERRAIN_ZONES`): a village (BUILDING) sitting on a hill (HIGH_GROUND) that the player holds, plus TREES zones in the open ground the enemy crosses. Elevation (0 = lowland, 1 = high ground) extends detection range for whoever holds it (+150px). BUILDING/TREES now separately affect concealment (spot chance) and cover (hit chance once spotted).
2. **Spotting.** Units are invisible to the opposing side (`Unit.is_spotted`) until detected. Detection is a per-tick probability roll (`CombatResolver.roll_spot`) based on range, elevation, terrain concealment, and whether the target is moving. Only spotted units can be fired upon (`Unit.is_targetable()`). No "losing" a spot once gained — deliberate simplification to keep state legible.
3. **Enemy strength increased.** Enemy is now 3 squads + 1 mortar vs. the player's 2 squads + 1 mortar — numeric superiority via unit count, not per-unit stat buffs, for legibility.
4. **After-action report, not win/lose.** Battle end now computes held-or-not (did the player avoid being fully routed) and an exchange ratio (enemy pips lost ÷ player pips lost), producing one of four verdicts: SUCCESSFUL DEFENSE, PYRRHIC DEFENSE, COSTLY WITHDRAWAL (favorable exchange even without holding), or DEFEAT. Thresholds (1.5:1 for a clean defense, 2.0:1 for a favorable withdrawal) are a first guess, not validated against actual play.
5. **Mortar model corrected.** Removed the mortar's retreat-threshold doctrine control entirely — a mortar crew (1 pip) is either in action or knocked out by a single hit, never worn down by percent casualties the way a squad is. A knocked-out mortar gets a flavor-only crew-casualty percentage (25-100%, random) for the AAR report; it has no mechanical effect.
6. **Defense advantages via movement + firing-reveals-position.** Enemy squads now physically advance from the map edge toward the village (straight-line, no pathfinding) until reaching engagement range, then hold or retreat; the enemy mortar stays put. Movement sets `Unit.Activity.MOVING`, tripling spot chance (`GameConfig.MOVING_SPOT_MULTIPLIER`). Firing a shot unconditionally reveals the firer's position (`is_spotted = true`) regardless of terrain, on both sides symmetrically.

**Verified before handoff:** a headless Godot simulation harness (written for verification, not shipped) ran the full battle loop end-to-end across multiple random seeds with no runtime errors, producing sensible and varied outcomes (holds, defeats, and a favorable-exchange withdrawal all observed).

**Not yet tuned:** all new numeric constants (detection range, spot chance, elevation bonus, enemy advance speed, AAR verdict thresholds) are first-pass estimates. Expect to adjust them after actually playing it.

### 2026-09-04 — v2 implementation feedback, six more changes

1. **Believable map.** Terrain now renders as an actual map instead of flat tinted rectangles: hills get contour-ring shading, the village gets individual building shapes with roofs, forests get a scattered tree-dot pattern, and a road cuts across the open ground (currently decorative only — no movement bonus yet). Shared between the battle view and the deployment screen via `GameConfig.draw_terrain()` so they always match.
2. **Drag-and-drop deployment.** Replaced the zone dropdown with `DeploymentScreen` — a real map view where you drag each of your 3 squads + mortar to a starting position inside `GameConfig.PLAYER_DEPLOYMENT_ZONE` (outlined in yellow) before the battle starts. Doctrine sliders (retreat threshold, mortar shoot-and-scoot) moved to a sidebar; `main.gd` combines both into the doctrine dict on Start Battle.
3. **General retreat order.** Added `BattleManager.order_general_retreat()`, wired to an "Order General Retreat" button visible throughout the battle. Forces every ACTIVE player unit into `Unit.State.RETREATING` immediately, regardless of casualties. Added a fourth unit state, `WITHDRAWN`, reached when a retreating unit (either side) physically reaches its own safe line — only then does it stop counting as "still fighting." Battle end now waits for all retreats to actually finish before generating the report.
4. **Stronger, sharper defense advantage.** A unit caught `MOVING` now loses its terrain cover entirely and takes fire at 1.6x the normal chance (`GameConfig.MOVING_HIT_MULTIPLIER`) — moving is genuinely dangerous, not just easier to spot. Enemy squads also get a two-tier casualty reaction: past a 25% casualty threshold they log "reports heavy resistance" (flavor only), past 50% they actually retreat.
5. **Fire log removed.** No more per-shot hit/miss entries. The log now only records spotting, revealing-by-fire, threshold/ordered retreats, reaching safety, going out of action, and counter-battery hits.
6. **Force ratio widened to 3 v 6.** Player: 3 squads + 1 mortar (was 2). Enemy: 6 squads + 1 mortar (was 3) — deliberately long odds. "Held" is now judged the instant no player unit remains ACTIVE (an ordered retreat gives up the position immediately, even before the withdrawal physically completes), while the AAR's exchange-ratio verdict tiers (unchanged from the v1 revision) already cover "retreated clean after inflicting casualties" as a TACTICAL WITHDRAWAL, separate from DEFEAT.

**Verified before handoff:** re-ran the headless simulation harness (written for verification, not shipped) across two scenarios — a full fight with no manual retreat, and one with a general retreat ordered mid-battle — confirming no runtime errors, correct WITHDRAWN transitions, casualties taken during withdrawal, and the concern-threshold flavor log firing correctly.

**Not yet verified:** the actual drag-and-drop interaction (mouse-down/motion/up on the deployment screen) — headless automation doesn't simulate real input well, so this needs eyes-on testing. All new constants (moving-hit multiplier, concern threshold, retreat speeds/safe lines, deployment zone bounds) are first-pass estimates.
