# Realism anchors

The user's standing instruction for the combat model: "as much as possible to keep the game results close to the real world". This ledger
lists the real-world figures the model is held to, where each came from, what the game does, and what is not modelled. A change to
the mortar, blast, casualty or movement code should not move the game out of a band below without a decision to do so.

* Unit-level anchors are asserted in `scripts/tests/test_realism_anchors.gd` (in the suite). A failure there means a change has moved the
  model out of a real-world band.
* Battle-level anchors are measured by `tools/research/realism_anchors_sim.gd` (about 15 minutes; copy under `scripts/` to run), with the
  bands in its header. Run it after a change to who fights whom, fire control or movement, and record the result here.
* Sources are mostly secondary (Wikipedia's summaries of the analysts and of doctrine, 2026-10); the manuals themselves were not reached.
  A figure marked JUDGMENT has no source found.

## Not anchored (no real-world figure found)
* Researched 2026-10-18, modelled 2026-10-18 on the user's rulings (doctrine entry of that date): **how few people can serve a mortar, and how fast they fire.** Found: crews are 3 (60 mm M224), 4 (2B14 Podnos, 82 mm), 5 (M252 81 mm; Soltam K6 120 mm) (Wikipedia); "a mortar squad consists of three to five mortarmen, depending on the system" and the roles per round are the gunner (lays the sight and fires), the assistant gunner (loads, helps lay), an ammunition bearer (prepares the charge and fuze, passes the round) and the squad leader (FM 23-90, 2000); the 60 mm can also be fired handheld by one soldier (FM 23-90; Wikipedia M224); the tube is carried as three loads (barrel, bipod mount, baseplate: the M252's are 16, 12 and 13 kg) and the 60 mm by a three-man carry (FM 7-90); rated fire is for a full crew (2B14: 24-30 rounds a minute; M252: 15 sustained, 30 for two minutes; M224: 20 sustained). The manuals say **nothing** about a reduced crew's rate of fire or a minimum crew; FM 7-90 only says understrength squads can be consolidated and rested and that "under extreme circumstances" a commander may leave a mortar behind so its ammunition can be carried for the other. So the game's figures are the user's: a hard minimum of two to fire; two cannot move the gun and shoot and hide; three move it at 70% with 30 rounds, rising to full pace with none; the cycle and the set-up time go as the share of the crew serving (JUDGMENT).
* Researched 2026-10-18 and modelled as the user ruled: **whether walking wounded can serve a crew.** Found: a casualty is "any person lost to a unit", "not someone who sustains injuries which do not prevent them from fighting" (Wikipedia, Casualty (person)), so those who stay at their post are not casualties at all, which is how the game's casualty roll already works; the walking wounded are the low-priority triage class, conscious and able to walk, who "may sometimes be called upon to assist treatment of more seriously injured patients or assist with other tasks" (Wikipedia, Walking wounded). The user: "if they are defined as lost to the unit, treat them as not participating." So they do not serve the gun. The game's 30% killed / 35% heavily wounded / 35% walking wounded split of casualties is a first-pass estimate; a real split was not researched.
* Researched 2026-10-18: **what a partial crew does about counter-battery.** FM 7-90, 7-1: countermortar fire can follow radio direction-finding "within two to six minutes"; sound ranging locates a firing mortar to about 100 m, visual observers to under 50 m from 3 km; 7-11 shoot-and-hide positions (the crew waits in a covered hide position and comes to the gun for a mission, baseplates and aiming posts left in the shoot position); 7-14 frequent displacement (survivability against responsiveness and fatigue); a roving gun; positions up to 300 m apart. Nothing was found on a partial crew's displacement, or on whether it is out of the battle. The game's counter-battery lands about 75-165 s after a shot (30-110 s to locate, 5-15 s to lay, 40 s of flight).
